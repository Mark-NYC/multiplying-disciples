<ArticleIntro>The gospel is the good news that Jesus, God's Son, died for our sins and rose from the dead, so that everyone who turns from their sin and trusts him is forgiven, brought back to God, and given new life under his reign. The word means good news. It is an announcement of something God has done, not a list of things you must do to earn his approval.</ArticleIntro>

That last part is the piece people miss. Most of us hear "gospel" and expect a set of rules or a religion to keep. But the gospel is news, the way a report of a rescue is news. Something happened, in real history, that changes everything about your standing with God. Your job is to hear it and respond, not to pull it off yourself.

## What the Word Actually Means

"Gospel" translates a Greek word, euangelion, that simply means good news or a good announcement. In the ancient world it was the word for the report a herald brought back from a battle: the victory is won, the enemy is defeated, you can come home.

That background matters. The gospel is first an announcement about what God has done through Jesus, before it is ever an instruction about what you should do. Good advice tells you to go and try harder. Good news tells you what has already happened on your behalf. The gospel is the second kind of thing.

## The Gospel in One Sentence

If you want the shortest faithful summary in the Bible, Paul gives it:

<ScriptureBlock reference="1 Corinthians 15:3-4">
For what I received I passed on to you as of first importance: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures.
</ScriptureBlock>

Three facts carry the whole thing: Christ died for our sins, he was buried, and he was raised on the third day. Paul calls these matters "of first importance." Everything else the New Testament says about salvation hangs on those events actually happening.

But a single sentence assumes a story. To feel the weight of the good news, you have to see the four movements it fits inside.

## The Story the Good News Fits Inside

<InsightCard label="Four Movements of the Gospel">
**God.** God made us, and he made us for himself, to know him and live under his good rule.

**Sin.** We have all turned away from God to run our own lives. That rebellion separates us from him and earns death (Romans 3:23, Romans 6:23).

**Jesus.** God sent his Son. Jesus lived the life we failed to live, died on the cross in our place, and rose from the dead, defeating sin and death.

**Response.** Everyone who turns from sin and trusts Jesus as Lord is forgiven, reconciled to God, and given new, unending life.
</InsightCard>

This is the same shape you can [draw as three circles](/the-three-circles-gospel-presentation-step-by-step/): God's good design, the brokenness our sin caused, and the rescue Jesus offers. Learn to sketch those three circles, and you can walk anyone through this story in a couple of minutes.

Notice where the good news lands. We could not climb back to God, so God came down to us. That is why the most famous verse in the Bible puts God's initiative first:

<ScriptureBlock reference="John 3:16">
For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life.
</ScriptureBlock>

## Why It Is Good News

The gospel is not only true. It is good, and it helps to be specific about what it actually gives you.

- **Forgiveness.** Your sins, all of them, are paid for at the cross and no longer counted against you.
- **Reconciliation.** The separation between you and God is removed. You are brought near, not kept at a distance.
- **New life.** God gives you his Spirit and makes you new from the inside, not just forgiven but changed.
- **Adoption.** You are not merely pardoned. You are welcomed as a son or daughter into God's family.
- **Hope that outlasts death.** Because Jesus rose, everyone in him will rise too. Death is not the end of the story.

None of this is earned. Paul is blunt that it comes by grace through faith, as a gift, and not by works, so that no one can boast (Ephesians 2:8-9). That is what makes it good news rather than good advice.

<ScriptureBlock reference="Romans 1:16">
For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God that brings salvation to everyone who believes: first to the Jew, then to the Gentile.
</ScriptureBlock>

## The Gospel Is That Jesus Is King, Not Only That You Are Forgiven

Here is where a lot of modern versions of the gospel get thin. We shrink it down to "trust Jesus so you can go to heaven when you die," as if it were mainly insurance against hell. Forgiveness and eternal life are gloriously real. But they sit inside something larger.

Jesus did not begin his ministry by announcing a way to heaven. He announced a kingdom:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Mark 1:15">
"The time has come," he said. "The kingdom of God has come near. Repent and believe the good news!"
</ScriptureBlock>

The good news is that God's rightful King has come, has beaten sin and death, and is now gathering people to live under his reign. Believing the gospel means more than agreeing to a transaction. It means bowing to Jesus as Lord and beginning to live as his. That is why turning to Jesus and following Jesus are never two separate steps in the New Testament. To trust the King is to start following the King.

## How to Respond to the Gospel

News like this calls for an answer. The Bible describes the response in a few plain words.

**Repent.** Turn from running your own life and from the sin that runs it. Change your mind about who is in charge.

**Believe.** Trust that Jesus died for your sins and rose again, and that this is enough. You are not adding your good works to his finished work.

**Confess Jesus as Lord.** Hand him the authority to run your life.

<ScriptureBlock reference="Romans 10:9">
If you declare with your mouth, "Jesus is Lord," and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved.
</ScriptureBlock>

The first believers made this response public right away through baptism. When the crowd at Pentecost asked what they should do, Peter said, "Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins" (Acts 2:38). If you have never responded to the gospel, you can turn to Jesus today, in your own words, and then tell another believer so they can help you take the next step.

<WarningCard label="What the Gospel Is Not">
**It is not "be a good person."** Moral effort is our attempt to save ourselves. The gospel is God saving us because we could not.

**It is not only going to heaven when you die.** That is one result of the gospel, not the whole of it. The gospel is Jesus as King, rescuing and ruling a people now.

**It is not your testimony.** Your story of what Jesus did in your life is powerful, but it points to the gospel. The gospel itself is the news about Jesus, true whether or not anyone has felt it yet.
</WarningCard>

<PullQuote>The gospel is not advice about how to reach God. It is news that God has reached us, in Jesus, and the only fitting response is to trust him and follow him as Lord.</PullQuote>

## Once You Know It, You Can Share It

If you can explain the gospel clearly to yourself, you can explain it to someone else. That is the whole point of getting it clear. The good news was never meant to be kept.

So take the next step. Learn to [share it in an ordinary conversation](/how-to-share-the-gospel/), tell people [your own story](/how-to-share-your-testimony/) of what Jesus has done, and keep [three simple circles](/3-circles/) ready so you always have a way to draw it out. And when someone responds, help them start following Jesus, because a real response to the gospel opens into [a whole new life as a disciple](/what-is-a-disciple/).

<PracticeCard label="Your Next Step">
1. **Say it in a minute.** Practice explaining the gospel out loud using the four movements above (God, sin, Jesus, response) until you can do it without notes.
2. **Name one person** who has never clearly heard it.
3. **Pray for them by name** this week, and ask God for an open door.
4. **If you have never responded yourself,** turn to Jesus today and tell a Christian you trust so they can help you take the next step.
</PracticeCard>

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the gospel in simple terms?

The gospel is the good news that Jesus, God's Son, died for our sins and rose from the dead, so that everyone who turns from sin and trusts him is forgiven and given new life under his reign. The word "gospel" means good news. It is an announcement of what God has done in Jesus, not a set of rules for earning God's approval (1 Corinthians 15:3-4).

### What are the main points of the gospel?

Most simple summaries follow four movements: God made us for himself; we all sinned and turned away from him; Jesus lived, died for our sins, and rose again; and everyone who repents and trusts him as Lord is forgiven and made new. The 3 Circles and similar tools are just ways of drawing this same story so you can share it.

### Is the gospel just about going to heaven when you die?

No. Eternal life is a real part of the good news, but the gospel is bigger than a ticket to heaven. Jesus announced that God's kingdom had come and called people to repent and follow him as King (Mark 1:15). The gospel is that Jesus is Lord, rescuing and ruling a people now, not only a promise about the afterlife.

### What is the difference between the gospel and my testimony?

The gospel is the news about Jesus (his death and resurrection for sinners), which is true whether or not anyone has experienced it. Your testimony is your personal story of what Jesus has done in your life. Your story is a great way to open a conversation, but it points to the gospel rather than replacing it, so learn to share both.

### How do I respond to the gospel?

Repent (turn from running your own life), believe (trust that Jesus died and rose for you), and confess him as Lord (Romans 10:9). The first Christians made that response public through baptism (Acts 2:38). If you have never done this, you can turn to Jesus in your own words today, then tell a believer so they can help you take the next steps of following him.
