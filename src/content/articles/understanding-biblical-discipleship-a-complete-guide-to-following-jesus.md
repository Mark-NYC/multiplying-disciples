## What is Discipleship? Defining the Biblical Concept

![A disciple-making leader teaching a small group.](/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/disciple-making-leader.webp)

The **definition of discipleship** emerges from Jesus's revolutionary approach to spiritual formation. At its core, **discipleship** means intentionally following Jesus Christ, learning His ways, and becoming transformed into His image.

This transformative journey extends far beyond mere religious education – it encompasses a complete lifestyle of devotion, learning, and replication.

The biblical term for **discipleship** (mathētēs in Greek) carries rich meaning beyond simple following. Originally, it described an apprentice who learned not just information, but an entire way of life from their master.

**Jesus** elevated this concept by establishing the most comprehensive **definition of discipleship** ever seen: total commitment to Him and His model in the gospels that reshapes every aspect of life.

### Core Elements of Biblical Discipleship

- Personal relationship with **Jesus** through regular prayer and Bible study
- Intentional character transformation to reflect Christ's nature
- Active participation in a faith community for [accountability](/unlock-the-power-of-friendly-accountability/) and growth
- Regular practice of spiritual disciplines like fasting and worship
- Multiplication through training others to be and make disciples

### Key Discipleship Quotes from the Bible:

- "If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me" (Luke 9:23)
- "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations" (Matthew 28:19)
- "A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone when he is fully trained will be like his teacher" (Luke 6:40)

## The Cost of Discipleship

**Jesus** never hid the price tag. Before anyone signed up to follow Him, He told two short stories about counting the cost first — a builder who checks he can finish the tower, a king who checks he can win the war (Luke 14:28-32). Skipping that math doesn't make **discipleship** easier; it just guarantees you'll quit partway through, confused about why following Jesus feels harder than advertised.

This is the piece a lot of modern **discipleship** teaching quietly drops. It's easier to talk about the benefits of following Jesus than the cost of it. But **Jesus** was specific: "Whoever does not carry their own cross and follow me cannot be my disciple" (Luke 14:27), and even more bluntly, "Whoever does not renounce all that he has cannot be my disciple" (Luke 14:33).

### Cheap Grace vs. Costly Grace

The theologian Dietrich Bonhoeffer named this tension directly in his book *The Cost of Discipleship*, written as he watched the German church accommodate itself to Nazism rather than pay any real price for following Christ. His distinction still cuts today:

- **Cheap grace** is forgiveness treated as a doctrine to affirm rather than a call to follow — grace without a cross, faith without obedience, a Savior with no Lordship attached.
- **Costly grace** is the real thing: grace that's free because Christ paid for it, but that costs the disciple everything, because it calls him to actually follow.

Bonhoeffer's most famous line on this — "when Christ calls a man, he bids him come and die" — isn't about literal martyrdom for most disciples. It's about the daily death of self-rule that Luke 9:23 calls taking up your cross "daily."

### What the Cost Actually Looks Like

| Area of Life | What Cheap Discipleship Keeps | What Costly Discipleship Surrenders |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Whatever's left over after other priorities | A real, protected place for abiding and obeying |
| Relationships | Comfortable distance from people who need the gospel | Real investment in a few, even when it's inconvenient |
| Reputation | Approval of people who don't follow Jesus | Willingness to be misunderstood for obedience |
| Control | The right to decide your own direction | Following even when the next step isn't fully clear |

### Practical Steps for Counting the Cost

- Name the one area above where you're currently paying "cheap" instead of "costly" — be specific, not general
- Ask a mentor or accountability partner to name what they see, since blind spots are rarely self-diagnosed
- Take one concrete, costly step of obedience this week rather than resolving to "try harder" in the abstract
- Revisit Luke 14:25-33 before starting a Discovery Bible Study group, so newer believers count the cost from day one instead of discovering it later and feeling misled

Counting the cost isn't a warning to stay away from **discipleship** — it's the doorway into a **discipleship** that's actually real, and durable enough to survive hardship instead of evaporating at the first cost it meets.

## Core Elements of Biblical Discipleship

True biblical **discipleship** rests on three foundational pillars: knowledge, obedience, and sharing. While Western Christianity often overemphasizes knowledge acquisition, authentic **discipleship** requires equal growth in putting truth into practice and passing it on to others. This balanced approach reflects **Jesus's** method of developing disciples who both know and live out their faith.

### The Knowledge-Obedience-Sharing Triangle

The essence of **discipleship** emerges when knowledge transforms into action and multiplication. Each element reinforces the others:

- Knowledge provides the foundation for faithful obedience
- Obedience turns knowledge into life-changing experience
- Sharing deepens both understanding and commitment

| Element | Biblical Principle | Common Imbalance | Healthy Practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | 2 Timothy 3:16-17 | Information without application | Study for transformation |
| Obedience | James 1:22 | Knowing without doing | Immediate application |
| Sharing | 2 Timothy 2:2 | Private faith journey | Regular multiplication |

### Beyond Knowledge: The Practice of Discipleship

**Jesus** taught that true **discipleship** requires implementation:

- Practice new truths within 24 hours of learning them
- Share insights with others to reinforce learning
- Create accountability for applying Scripture
- Focus on small, consistent steps of obedience
- Measure growth by transformation, not information

### Key Discipleship Scriptures on Balanced Growth

- "Be doers of the word, and not hearers only" (James 1:22)
- "If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them" (John 13:17)
- "Teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you" (Matthew 28:20)

### Practical Steps for Balanced Discipleship

Knowledge Application:

- Study with specific application in mind
- Write down one action step from each study
- Focus on depth over breadth in Scripture study
- Connect every truth to daily life

Obedience Growth:

- Start small but start immediately
- Create regular practice routines
- Find accountability partners
- Celebrate steps of obedience

Sharing Development:

- Share each new lesson with someone else
- Practice explaining biblical concepts simply
- Look for daily opportunities to multiply
- Start discipling others while still growing

Action Steps for Balanced Growth:

- Identify your strongest and weakest areas among knowledge, obedience, and sharing
- Create specific plans to strengthen weak areas
- Find accountability for regular practice
- Begin sharing what you're learning immediately
- Develop simple ways to pass on truth to others

True **discipleship** flourishes when knowledge, obedience, and sharing grow together. This balanced approach produces disciples who not only know the truth but live it out and effectively pass it on to others.
