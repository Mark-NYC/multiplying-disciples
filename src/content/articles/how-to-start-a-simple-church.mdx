When people ask how to start a simple church, they usually picture a launch: a living room set up like a small sanctuary, a start date, a plan for music and a talk. Set that picture down. A simple church is not a meeting you launch. It is a spiritual family that has Christ as King in loving God, loving others, and making disciples. You do not manufacture a family by scheduling a service. You grow one by making a few disciples and gathering them around Jesus.

## Start with disciples, not a service

Here is the shift that changes everything: a simple church is the fruit of disciple-making, not the starting point of it. In the New Testament, churches appeared where the gospel had gone and people had believed. First there were disciples, then there was a church, in that order.

So the first move in starting a simple church is not to book a room. It is to disciple a few people, usually the ones already around you. If you have never done that, [one-on-one discipleship](/one-on-one-discipleship/) walks the whole path: you help a person start reaching the people in their own world, open the Bible with those who are seeking, and watch some of them come to faith. A simple church is what it looks like when a few of those new believers start following Jesus together.

## You do not need permission, you need obedience

A lot of people stall here because they assume starting a church requires ordination, a denomination's blessing, or a qualified pastor in the room. It does not. Jesus said where his people gather in his name, he is there.

<ScriptureBlock reference="Matthew 18:20">
For where two or three gather in my name, there am I with them.
</ScriptureBlock>

That is the charter of every simple church. You are not waiting for a credential or a building. You are gathering people around the living presence of Jesus and helping them obey what he said. This is not a loophole; it is how the church actually began, in homes, around tables, led by ordinary people who loved Jesus. If you can make a disciple, you can help start a church.

If you want a simple picture of what you are actually forming, we use a handy guide to church you can count on one hand: the five W's of Acts 2:36-47, covered in full in [what makes a church a church](/what-makes-a-church-a-church/). Who (baptized believers who have turned to Jesus), What (the practices of a church), Where (house to house), When (a daily, relational rhythm), and Why (to host God's presence). Keep that hand in view as you start, and you will know what you are aiming at.

## A simple path to start

You do not need a complicated plan. You need a few reproducible moves you could hand to the next person, so the church you start is able to start another. Here is the shape.

<InsightCard label="How to start a simple church">
1. **Make a few disciples.** Reach the people already in your life, open the [7 Stories of Hope](https://obey.tools/stories-of-hope.html) with those who are seeking, and walk with them toward faith.

2. **Gather them as a family.** As they believe, bring them together around Jesus and help them commit to one another, not just to a meeting. This is where a group becomes a church.

3. **Give it a rhythm.** Meet with a simple, repeatable format like [Three Thirds](/three-thirds/): look back (worship, care, accountability), look up (a passage to obey), look ahead (practice, goals, prayer).

4. **Grow in obedience together.** Learn to obey the [commands of Christ](/what-are-the-commands-of-christ/) as a family, using the [Church Waffle](/movement-resources/12-practice-church-circle/) for the What of the handy guide: the twelve practices of a healthy church, drawn from Acts 2:36-47.

5. **Plan to multiply from day one.** Expect that some in your church will start reaching their own people, and that a new simple church will be born one relationship over.
</InsightCard>

None of these steps needs an expert. That is the point. Every part of this is simple enough that the people you gather could turn around and do it themselves.

## Give it a rhythm it can reproduce

The thing that keeps a simple church from drifting into a weekly hangout is a rhythm built around obeying Scripture together. That is what the [Three Thirds](/three-thirds/) format is for. It is not a program; it is a shape that makes sure every gathering moves from hearing to doing: you look back at how you obeyed and cared for one another, look up at one passage simple enough for a new believer to lead, and look ahead by naming what each person will actually do before you meet again.

Keep it that simple on purpose. The more complexity you add, the fewer people can lead it, and a church that only you can run is a church that cannot multiply.

## Keep it reproducible from day one

The measure of a healthy simple church is not how full the room gets. It is whether it can start another one. So build reproducibility in from the beginning. Use tools anyone can pick up, keep the meeting simple enough for a new believer to lead, and from the start expect the people you gather to reach their own oikos.

<ScriptureBlock reference="2 Timothy 2:2">
And the things you have heard me say in the presence of many witnesses entrust to reliable people who will also be qualified to teach others.
</ScriptureBlock>

A simple church that keeps handing off what it has received does not just grow. It reproduces, and that is the whole point of starting one.

Starting the church is only the left hand of the work. There is a right hand too, [a handy guide to church health](/what-makes-a-church-healthy/), for helping a young church grow toward maturity. Between the two is the messy middle: the real, often bumpy stretch where a new gathering is learning to become a healthy church. Do not be surprised by that middle, and do not try to skip it. It is where a church start actually grows up, and [when a discipleship group becomes a church](/when-a-discipleship-group-becomes-a-church/) walks through it. Remember that health is not something you organize into place. You start simply, and it emerges over time as the church takes hold of Jesus for itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a simple church?

Start by making a few disciples rather than launching a meeting. Reach the people already around you, open the Bible with those who are seeking, and as some come to faith, gather them as a spiritual family committed to Jesus and to one another. Give that family a simple, reproducible rhythm like Three Thirds, learn to obey the commands of Christ together, and expect from the beginning that the church will multiply as its members reach their own people.

### Do I need to be ordained to start a church?

No. A simple church is a group of believers gathered around Jesus, and Jesus himself said that where two or three gather in his name, he is there (Matthew 18:20). The first churches met in homes and were led by ordinary believers. You do not need a title, a building, or a denomination's permission to gather people around Jesus and help them obey him. You need to be a disciple who is willing to make disciples.

### How many people do you need to start a church?

Fewer than most people think. Jesus set the floor at two or three gathered in his name (Matthew 18:20). A simple church often begins with one family or a small handful of new believers. What matters is not the size of the group but whether Christ is King among them in loving God, loving others, and making disciples, and whether the group is built to reproduce rather than just to gather.
