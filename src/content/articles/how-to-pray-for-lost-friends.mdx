That is the short answer, and it is more doable than it sounds. Most of us carry a vague weight for people we love who do not know Jesus, but the weight rarely turns into actual prayer. It stays a feeling. The way through is to make it specific, write it down, and stop carrying it alone.

## Start With Names, Not a Feeling

"I should pray for lost people" is too big to pray. You cannot picture "lost people," so you end up praying nothing. Names change that.

Write down a few real people. Your brother. The coworker two desks over. The neighbor you wave to. The friend from before you followed Jesus. These are the people already in your life, what the Bible calls your [oikos](/what-is-an-oikos/), your natural web of relationships. God put them around you on purpose, and they are the most natural place to start. Keep the list short. Three to five names you pray for every day beats thirty you glance at once a week.

## Track Each Name in a Conversation Box

A list is a good start, but a list forgets. You lose track of who you talked to, who is warming up, who you have not thought about in a month. So we do not just list people, we track them, using a simple tool called the [Conversation Box](https://obey.tools/conversation-box.html).

The box holds each person's name where the conversation actually is right now, and you follow each name on two fronts:

- **Share.** Where is the conversation? The box moves from **casual** to **meaningful** to **spiritual** to **discovery**, reading the Bible together. You put each person in the square that fits today, and you move them forward as real conversations deepen. It takes the mystery out of "how do I even bring up Jesus," because you can see the next honest step.
- **Prayer.** How are you praying for this person? Every name in the box is a name you lift up. The box keeps them in front of you so nobody quietly falls off your heart.

<InsightCard label="Two fronts, one name">
For every person in your box you are tracking two things: how you are praying for them, and how the conversation is moving from casual toward discovery. Prayer and sharing are not two separate jobs. They are the two sides of loving one person toward Jesus.
</InsightCard>

Seeing it laid out does something to you. A name you have not moved in weeks is a nudge to reach out. A name inching from casual to meaningful is an encouragement to keep going. The box turns a vague burden into a clear, prayable, doable picture.

## The Prayer Side: What to Actually Pray

Once a name is in the box, you may not know what to say past "God, save them." Here is what the Bible points you toward.

**Ask God to open their hearts.** People do not come to Jesus by being argued into it. Something has to open in them, and that is God's work:

<ScriptureBlock reference="2 Corinthians 4:4">
The god of this age has blinded the minds of unbelievers, so that they cannot see the light of the gospel that displays the glory of Christ, who is the image of God.
</ScriptureBlock>

So pray that God would open blind eyes and soften hard ground, the things you cannot do yourself.

**Ask God to open doors, and to give you courage.** Paul, who feared nothing, still asked people to pray for open doors and the words to say (Colossians 4:3). Pray the same over each name: that a door would open, and that you would be ready to walk through it.

**Ask God to send other believers into their lives.** You are not the only one God can use. Jesus told us to ask him to send workers into the harvest (Matthew 9:37-38), so pray that your friend keeps running into people who follow Jesus, not only you.

And pray plainly for what you most want: that they would meet Jesus, trust him, and follow him. God wants this even more than you do. He "wants all people to be saved and to come to a knowledge of the truth" (1 Timothy 2:4).

## Pray Together and Celebrate by Name

Here is where this stops being a private discipline and becomes something better. Do not keep your box to yourself. Bring it to your group.

When we gather, we pray through our boxes out loud, by name. We ask God for the people who are still casual, we thank him for the ones moving toward spiritual conversations, and when someone crosses a line, has their first real spiritual conversation, opens a Bible, trusts Christ, we say their name and we celebrate. Out loud. Together.

That is not a gimmick. It is how heaven works:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Luke 15:10">
In the same way, I tell you, there is rejoicing in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.
</ScriptureBlock>

Praying together by name does three things a private list never will. It keeps you going, because other people are asking about your names. It multiplies the prayer, because now a room is praying for your brother, not just you. And it turns evangelism from a lonely weight into shared joy, because you get to celebrate what God is doing in real people you all know by name. The early church prayed like this, devoted to it together (Acts 2:42), and joy was the normal result.

## Keep Going

Praying someone toward Jesus is usually slow. People often take years, and along the way it can look like nothing is happening. That is exactly where most of us quietly stop. Jesus told a whole parable so we would not:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Luke 18:1">
Then Jesus told his disciples a parable to show them that they should always pray and not give up.
</ScriptureBlock>

Keep the name in the box. Keep praying. Remember that God is patient with them the way he was patient with you, and that the growth is his to give. You plant and water; God makes things grow (1 Corinthians 3:6-7). Your job is to keep asking, keep loving them, keep taking the next honest step, and keep bringing the name back to the group.

As you pray and watch, stay alert for the person who seems unusually open, the one the Bible calls a [person of peace](/finding-persons-of-peace-a-key-strategy-for-evangelism-and-disciple-making/). When a door opens, you will want to know what to say, and [how to share the gospel](/how-to-share-the-gospel/) walks you through it simply. Discipling one person all the way is what [one-on-one discipleship](/one-on-one-discipleship/) is for.

<WarningCard label="A box is not a scoreboard">
The point of tracking people is love, not performance. The squares help you pray and remember, they are not levels to grind or a way to rank your friends by how close they are to "converting." Do not pressure someone to advance so your box looks better, and do not treat a name as a project. Celebrate God's work and the person, keep loving people who never move a square, and remember that praying and tracking are never a substitute for actually showing up in someone's life.
</WarningCard>

<PracticeCard label="Start This Week">
1. **Put three to five names in the box.** People you already know who do not yet follow Jesus. Place each one where the conversation honestly is: casual, meaningful, spiritual, or discovery.
2. **Pray and take one step.** For each name, ask God to open their heart, open a door, and give you courage. Then take one small step toward one person this week: a text, a question, an invitation.
3. **Bring your box to your group.** Pray through your names together, and when someone moves forward, say their name and celebrate it out loud.
</PracticeCard>

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I pray for a friend who doesn't believe?

Pray for them by name, and keep it specific. Ask God to open their heart and their eyes (2 Corinthians 4:4), to bring other believers into their life (Matthew 9:38), to open a door for the gospel, and to give you courage when the moment comes. Tracking each name in a Conversation Box helps you pray consistently and see where the conversation is, so prayer and sharing move together.

### What does the Bible say about praying for the lost?

It tells us to do it and that God wants it. Paul urges that "petitions, prayers, intercession and thanksgiving be made for all people," because God "wants all people to be saved" (1 Timothy 2:1-4). Jesus tells us to ask the Lord of the harvest to send out workers (Matthew 9:37-38) and to always pray and not give up (Luke 18:1). And heaven celebrates when one person turns to God (Luke 15:10), which is why we celebrate by name too.

### How long should I pray for a lost friend?

As long as it takes, and without giving up. People often come to Jesus over years, not days, and the stretch where nothing seems to change is where most people stop praying. Keep the name in your box, keep it in front of your group, and keep asking. God was patient with you, and the growth is his to give (1 Corinthians 3:6-7).

### What if my prayers feel like nothing is changing?

That feeling is normal and not a reason to quit. Much of what God is doing in a person is invisible to you, and hearts usually turn slowly. Keep praying, keep loving them, and watch for small openings. Praying with a group helps here: other people can see progress you have missed, and their encouragement keeps you going when your own patience runs thin.
