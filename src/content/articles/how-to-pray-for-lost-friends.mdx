That is the short answer, and it is more doable than it sounds. Most of us carry a vague weight for people we love who do not know Jesus, but the weight rarely turns into actual prayer. It stays a feeling. The fix is to get specific.

## Start With Names, Not a Feeling

"I should pray for lost people" is too big to pray. You cannot picture "lost people," so you end up praying nothing. Names change that.

Write down a few real people. Your brother. The coworker two desks over. The neighbor you wave to. The friend from before you followed Jesus. These are the people already in your life, what the Bible calls your [oikos](/what-is-an-oikos/), your natural web of relationships. God put them around you on purpose, and they are the most natural place to start.

Keep the list short. Three to five names you will actually pray for every day beats thirty you glance at once a week and feel bad about. You can always add more as God grows your heart for them.

<InsightCard label="The whole practice in one line">
Write down a few people you know who don't yet follow Jesus, pray for them by name every day, and watch for the doors God opens.
</InsightCard>

## What to Actually Pray

Once you have names, you may not know what to say past "God, save them." Here is what the Bible points you toward.

**Ask God to open their hearts.** People do not come to Jesus by being argued into it. Something has to open in them, and that is God's work:

<ScriptureBlock reference="2 Corinthians 4:4">
The god of this age has blinded the minds of unbelievers, so that they cannot see the light of the gospel that displays the glory of Christ, who is the image of God.
</ScriptureBlock>

So pray that God would open blind eyes and soften hard ground, the things you cannot do yourself.

**Ask God to open doors, and to give you courage.** Paul, who feared nothing, still asked people to pray for open doors and for the words to say. Ask for the same:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Colossians 4:3">
And pray for us, too, that God may open a door for our message, so that we may proclaim the mystery of Christ.
</ScriptureBlock>

**Ask God to send other believers into their lives.** You are not the only one God can use. Jesus told us to ask him to send workers into the harvest (Matthew 9:37-38), so pray that your lost friend keeps running into people who follow Jesus, not only you.

And pray plainly for what you most want: that they would meet Jesus, trust him, and follow him. God wants this even more than you do. He "wants all people to be saved and to come to a knowledge of the truth" (1 Timothy 2:4). When you pray for a lost friend, you are praying for something God already desires.

## Pray With Expectation, and Keep Going

Praying for someone to come to Jesus is usually not quick. People often take years, and along the way it can look like nothing is happening. That is exactly the point where most of us quietly stop.

Jesus told a whole parable so we would not:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Luke 18:1">
Then Jesus told his disciples a parable to show them that they should always pray and not give up.
</ScriptureBlock>

Keep going. Keep the name on the list. Remember that God is patient with them the same way he was patient with you, and that the growth is his to give. You plant and water; God makes things grow (1 Corinthians 3:6-7). Your job is to keep asking, keep loving them, and keep watching.

## Let Your Prayers Move You

Here is what often happens when you pray for someone by name every day: God starts to make you part of the answer. You notice them more. You care more. You see an opening you would have walked past before.

That is normal, and it is good. Prayer is not a way to hand the job to God so you do not have to do anything. It is usually how God gets you ready to act. As you pray, watch for the person who seems unusually open, the one the Bible calls a [person of peace](/finding-persons-of-peace-a-key-strategy-for-evangelism-and-disciple-making/), and be ready to take the next step when a door opens. When it does, you will want to know what to say, and [how to share the gospel](/how-to-share-the-gospel/) walks you through it simply.

<WarningCard label="Prayer is not a place to hide">
Praying for lost friends is essential, and it is not a substitute for ever opening your mouth. Some of us pray for years precisely so we never have to risk a conversation. Prayer and going belong together: we pray because we cannot change a heart, and we go because God has chosen to reach people through people. Do not let the list become a way to feel faithful while staying safe.
</WarningCard>

<PracticeCard label="Start This Week">
1. **Write three to five names.** People you already know who do not yet follow Jesus. Keep it short enough to pray daily.
2. **Set a daily trigger.** Attach the prayer to something you already do, your morning coffee or commute, or set an alarm. For each name, ask God to open their heart, to open a door, and to give you courage.
3. **Watch and take one step.** Notice who seems open, and this week take one small step toward one of them: a text, a question, an invitation, a kindness.
</PracticeCard>

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I pray for a friend who doesn't believe?

Pray for them by name, and keep it specific. Ask God to open their heart and their eyes (2 Corinthians 4:4), to bring other believers into their life (Matthew 9:38), to open a door for the gospel, and to give you courage and words when the moment comes. Then pray plainly that they would come to trust and follow Jesus. Short and daily beats long and occasional.

### What does the Bible say about praying for the lost?

It tells us to do it and that God wants it. Paul urges that "petitions, prayers, intercession and thanksgiving be made for all people," because God "wants all people to be saved" (1 Timothy 2:1-4). Jesus tells us to ask the Lord of the harvest to send out workers (Matthew 9:37-38) and to always pray and not give up (Luke 18:1). Paul also asks others to pray for open doors for the message (Colossians 4:3).

### How long should I pray for a lost friend?

As long as it takes, and without giving up. People often come to Jesus over years, not days, and the stretch where nothing seems to change is where most people stop praying. Keep the name on your list. God was patient with you, and he is patient with them, and the growth is his to give (1 Corinthians 3:6-7).

### What if my prayers feel like nothing is changing?

That feeling is normal and not a reason to quit. Much of what God is doing in a person is invisible to you, and hearts usually turn slowly. Keep praying, keep loving them, and watch for small openings. Often God uses the very prayers that feel pointless to change you, and to make you part of the answer you have been asking for.
