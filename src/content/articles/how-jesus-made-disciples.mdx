It is a pattern he expected his followers to repeat, which means it is a pattern you can follow. And when you look at how he actually did it, the most striking thing is how ordinary and unimpressive the method was.

## He Called Ordinary People

Jesus did not recruit the qualified. He walked up to working men at their jobs and called them:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Mark 1:16-18">
As Jesus walked beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and his brother Andrew casting a net into the lake, for they were fishermen. "Come, follow me," Jesus said, "and I will send you out to fish for people." At once they left their nets and followed him.
</ScriptureBlock>

Fishermen. Later a tax collector. Not scholars, not religious leaders, not the people you would have picked to change the world. That is the point. Jesus made disciples out of available people, not impressive ones. If you have ever thought you are not qualified to disciple anyone, you are exactly the kind of person he built his whole plan around.

## "Follow Me, and I Will Make You"

Notice the order in that first call. Following comes first, and the competence comes from him. "Follow me, and I will make you fishers of people." They did not have to become qualified before they could come. They came, and he made them into something over time.

This matters for how disciples are still made. You do not wait until you have it all together to follow Jesus, and the people you disciple do not wait until they are experts to start. Following him is the beginning, and he does the making along the way.

<PullQuote>Jesus made disciples out of available people, not impressive ones. He built his whole plan around the kind of person who feels unqualified.</PullQuote>

## With Him, and Sent Out

Here is the heart of the method, and it is one verse:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Mark 3:14">
He appointed twelve that they might be with him and that he might send them out to preach.
</ScriptureBlock>

Two things at once: with him, and sent out. That is the whole shape of how Jesus made disciples. He did not sit them in a lecture hall for three years and then graduate them. He shared his life with them, day after day, and he sent them out to actually do the work while they were still learning. They watched him heal, teach, and reach people, and then he sent them to try it themselves.

<InsightCard label="The pattern in one look">
Jesus called ordinary people to **follow**, kept them **with him** so they could catch how he lived, **sent them out** to practice while they were still learning, taught them to **obey** what he commanded, and trained them to do all of it **with others**. Simple enough to reproduce, which is the point.
</InsightCard>

This is why disciples are still best made through shared life and real practice, not information alone. You cannot hand someone a book and produce a disciple. It takes being with people and sending them to do the thing.

## He Trained Them, Then Released Them

Jesus gave his disciples real responsibility early, and he kept increasing it. He sent out the Twelve, and later seventy-two more, to do the very things they had watched him do, and they came back and talked through what happened:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Luke 10:17">
The seventy-two returned with joy and said, "Lord, even the demons submit to us in your name."
</ScriptureBlock>

That rhythm, watch me, now you try it, come back and let's talk, is how a skill actually transfers from one person to another. It is the pattern later summarized as model, assist, watch, and leave, and it is the backbone of [how to train disciples](/how-to-train-disciples/). Jesus was always moving his followers from watching to doing, because his aim was never to keep them dependent on him. It was to release them.

## He Aimed at Multiplication

Jesus was never making just twelve disciples. He was making disciples who would make disciples, in every nation, until the end of the age. His final command says so plainly:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Matthew 28:19-20">
Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you.
</ScriptureBlock>

Teaching them to obey, not just to know. And "make disciples," who then make more. He built the whole thing to reproduce beyond himself, poured his life into a few, and trusted that pattern to reach the world. Two thousand years later, it did, and it still does, one ordinary follower at a time. That is what [what is a disciple](/what-is-a-disciple/) means when it says a disciple is someone who follows Jesus, is changed by him, and helps others do the same.

## You Can Make Disciples the Same Way

Here is the good news underneath all of this. Jesus' method was not a special technique that only the Son of God could pull off. It was a simple, shareable pattern he handed to fishermen so they could hand it to others. Which means you can do it too.

You do not need a platform or a degree. You need to follow Jesus, share your life with a person or two, help them start obeying what he said, and send them to do the same with someone else. If you want the practical, step-by-step version of that pattern for a real person in your life, that is exactly what [how to make disciples](/how-to-make-disciples/) walks through. The way Jesus made disciples is the way you can, starting this week.

<WarningCard label="Do not overcomplicate it">
It is easy to turn disciple-making into something complicated and professional, and to conclude you are not ready. Jesus did the opposite. He kept it simple enough that fishermen could reproduce it. If your plan to make disciples requires expertise you do not have, it is probably more complicated than his was. Follow him, take someone with you, and help them do what you are doing. That is the method.
</WarningCard>

<PracticeCard label="Start This Week">
1. **Name one or two people.** Who could you actually share life with and help follow Jesus, the way Jesus did with a few?
2. **Do something with them, not just for them.** Read a Gospel story together, pray, or take them along when you serve or share. Let them watch and then try.
3. **Point to a next step.** Help them obey one thing Jesus said this week, and start showing them how to do the same with someone else.
</PracticeCard>

## Frequently asked questions

### How did Jesus make disciples?

He called ordinary people to follow him, kept them with him so they could catch how he lived, sent them out to practice while they were still learning, taught them to obey what he commanded rather than just know it, and trained them to do the same with others. It was shared life and real practice, summed up in Mark 3:14: he appointed them "that they might be with him and that he might send them out."

### What was Jesus' method of discipleship?

Life-on-life training aimed at reproduction. Jesus modeled ministry while his disciples watched, sent them to do it themselves and debriefed what happened (Luke 9-10), and steadily released more responsibility until they could carry it without him. The goal was never to keep them dependent, but to make disciples who would make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:19-20).

### Who did Jesus choose as disciples?

Ordinary people, not religious elites: fishermen like Peter, Andrew, James, and John, a tax collector named Matthew, and others with no special credentials. See the [twelve disciples in order](/discover-the-12-disciples-of-jesus-christ/) for who they each were. He deliberately built his plan out of available, unimpressive people, which is why anyone who follows him can be part of it.

### Can we make disciples the way Jesus did today?

Yes, because his method was simple and meant to be reproduced. You follow Jesus, share your life with a person or two, help them start obeying what he said, and send them to do the same with someone else. You do not need a platform or a degree. [How to make disciples](/how-to-make-disciples/) lays out that pattern step by step for a real person in your life.
