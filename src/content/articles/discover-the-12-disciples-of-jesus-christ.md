The **12 disciples of Jesus in order** weren’t chosen randomly. Jesus called each disciple intentionally—and understanding the **order in which Jesus called them** helps you see how His disciple-making pattern unfolds. If you’re looking for a clear list, a simple explanation, and an infographic showing the disciples in order of calling, this guide gives you everything in one place.

These weren’t religious elites. Jesus chose ordinary people, trained them, and sent them out to make more disciples—modeling a pattern still used today in disciple-making movements.

Below you’ll find the **12 disciples in order of calling**, along with notes, Bible references, and short biographies for each apostle.

## The 12 Disciples of Jesus in Order Called

1. **Andrew** (John 1:35-40) — One of the first two disciples to follow Jesus from John the Baptist
2. **John** (John 1:35-40) — Traditionally identified as John the son of Zebedee
3. **Peter** (John 1:41-42) — Andrew brings Peter to Jesus
4. **Philip** (John 1:43) — Jesus calls Philip the next day
5. **Nathanael (Bartholomew)** (John 1:45-51) — Called immediately after Philip
6. **James (son of Zebedee)** (Mark 1:19-20, Matthew 4:21-22) — Called with his brother John while fishing
7. **Matthew (Levi)** (Mark 2:13-14, Matthew 9:9, Luke 5:27-28) — All gospels place him definitively after fishermen calling
8. **Thomas (Didymus)** (Matthew 10:3, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:15)
9. **James son of Alphaeus** (Matthew 10:3, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:15)
10. **Thaddaeus (Judas son of James)** (Matthew 10:3, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:16) — Called Judas son of James
11. **Simon the Zealot** (Matthew 10:4, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:15)
12. **Judas Iscariot** (Matthew 10:4, Mark 3:19, Luke 6:16)

*Source: A.T. Robertson, A Harmony of the Gospels (Harper & Row, 1950)*

Who were the 12 disciples of Jesus in order of calling?

The 12 disciples of Jesus in order of their calling were: Andrew and John (first), Simon Peter (called through Andrew), Philip, Nathanael (Bartholomew), Matthew (Levi), James (son of Zebedee with John), Thomas, James (son of Alphaeus), Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot. After Judas's death, Matthias replaced him as the twelfth apostle.

Who was the 13th disciple?

Paul, originally known as Saul, is considered the 13th disciple of Jesus. Unlike the original twelve disciples who walked with Jesus during His earthly ministry, Paul encountered the risen Christ in a dramatic conversion on the road to Damascus, where a blinding light and Jesus' voice transformed him from a persecutor of Christians into one of the most influential apostles. He received his apostleship directly from Jesus Christ and went on to write 13 books of the New Testament, establishing many early churches throughout the Roman Empire. Though Matthias was chosen to replace Judas Iscariot among the twelve, Paul's extraordinary ministry and direct calling from Christ have led many to consider him the 13th disciple.

Which disciple betrayed Jesus?

Judas Iscariot betrayed Jesus to the chief priests for 30 pieces of silver. He identified Jesus to the arresting soldiers by kissing him in the Garden of Gethsemane. After the betrayal, consumed by guilt, Judas returned the money and took his own life.

Who was Jesus's first disciple?

Andrew was Jesus's first disciple, introduced to Jesus by John the Baptist (John 1:35-40). After spending time with Jesus, Andrew immediately found his brother Simon Peter and brought him to Jesus, saying "We have found the Messiah" (John 1:41-42). This makes Andrew both the first disciple and the first evangelist in the Gospels.

### Who were the 12 Disciples of Jesus Christ?

The 12 Disciples were a diverse group of men, each with their own unique backgrounds and experiences.

According to the Bible, Jesus handpicked these men to be his closest followers, giving them the name "apostles," which means "sent ones." The 12 Disciples were: Peter, Andrew, James (son of Zebedee), John, Philip, Bartholomew, Thomas, Matthew, James (son of Alphaeus), Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot.

These men were not necessarily religious leaders or scholars, but rather ordinary people who were willing to leave behind their old lives to follow Jesus. Their stories offer valuable lessons about the importance of faith, sacrifice, and service.

### The Legacy of the 12 Disciples of Jesus Christ

The 12 Disciples were also instrumental in the establishment of the early Christian church. After Jesus' death and resurrection, they continued his work, preaching his message and spreading the word of God throughout the world.

Their efforts laid the foundation for Christianity as we know it today.

But the legacy of the 12 Disciples goes beyond their individual stories. The way they were chosen, trained, and sent out has become a model for disciple making movements - a strategy for evangelism and church planting that emphasizes making disciples who make more disciples.

By following the example of the 12 Disciples and embracing the principles of disciple making movements, we can continue their work of spreading the message of Jesus Christ to all corners of the world.

Who are the 12 disciples of Jesus in order of calling

The 12 disciples of Jesus, in order of calling, are Simon Peter, Andrew, James (son of Zebedee), John, Philip, Bartholomew (also known as Nathanael), Matthew (also known as Levi), Thomas (also known as Didymus), James (son of Alphaeus), Thaddaeus (also known as Judas, son of James), Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot.

These names are mentioned in several passages in the Bible, including Matthew 10:1-4, Mark 3:13-19, Luke 6:12-16, and Acts 1:13. These verses provide insight into the specific order in which the disciples were called and named by Jesus.

It is important to note that the order in which the disciples are listed can vary slightly depending on the biblical passage being referenced.

Each of the disciples brought their unique skills, backgrounds, and perspectives to the group, and their individual stories are an important part of the larger narrative of the gospel.

## Simon Peter: The Rock of the Early Church

### Origins and Background

Simon Peter, originally named simply Simon, was born in the fishing village of Bethsaida on the Sea of Galilee. Born into a humble fishing family alongside his brother Andrew, Peter's life was deeply rooted in the daily rhythms of Galilean fishing culture. He later moved to Capernaum, where he operated a successful fishing business with his brother and the sons of Zebedee (James and John). He was married, as evidenced by Jesus healing his mother-in-law, suggesting he had significant family responsibilities alongside his apostolic calling.

### The Call to Discipleship

Peter's journey with Jesus began through his brother Andrew, a former disciple of John the Baptist. After a miraculous catch of fish that left him trembling before Jesus' divine power, Peter received his new name - Cephas (Aramaic) or Peter (Greek), meaning "rock." This renaming prophetically pointed to his future role in the church, though at the time, Peter was anything but rock-solid in his faith.

### Role in Jesus' Ministry

Throughout the Gospels, Peter emerges as a central figure in Jesus' inner circle:

- Present at the Transfiguration, witnessing Jesus' glory alongside James and John
- First to confess Jesus as "the Christ, the Son of the living God"
- Walked on water briefly before succumbing to doubt
- Witnessed the raising of Jairus' daughter
- Present in Gethsemane during Jesus' agony
- Attempted to defend Jesus with a sword in the garden
- Denied Jesus three times, fulfilling Jesus' prophecy
- First apostle to witness the empty tomb
- Received special post-resurrection appearance and restoration

### Leadership in the Early Church

The Book of Acts reveals Peter's transformation into a bold church leader:

1. Pentecost Leadership

- Delivered the first Christian sermon
- Led 3,000 people to faith in a single day
- Established patterns for Christian baptism and community

2. Miraculous Ministry

- Healed the lame man at the Beautiful Gate
- Raised Tabitha (Dorcas) from the dead
- His shadow was believed to have healing power
- Delivered from prison by an angel

3. Doctrinal Development

- Received vision about clean and unclean foods
- Pioneered Gentile inclusion through Cornelius' conversion
- Defended grace against legalism at Jerusalem Council

### Biblical Writings

Peter's contributions to Scripture include:

- First Peter: Written to persecuted Christians, emphasizing hope and holy living
- Second Peter: Addressing false teaching and encouraging spiritual growth
- Likely source for Mark's Gospel through his relationship with John Mark

### Traditional Impact and Legacy

Church tradition holds that Peter:

- Served as the first Bishop of Rome
- Was crucified upside-down under Nero (c. 64-67 AD)
- Requested inverted crucifixion, feeling unworthy to die like Jesus
- Established churches throughout Asia Minor
- Mentored Mark and influenced the second gospel
- His martyrdom site became location of St. Peter's Basilica

### Character Analysis

Peter's personality reveals a complex mixture of:

### Strengths

- Natural leadership abilities
- Quick to act and speak
- Passionate devotion to Jesus
- Willingness to take risks
- Ability to recover from failure
- Deep spiritual insights when inspired

### Growth Areas

- Tendency toward impulsiveness
- Struggled with fear and doubt
- Sometimes spoke before thinking
- Needed to learn humility
- Initially resistant to suffering

### Transformation

Peter's journey from fisherman to "fisher of men" demonstrates profound spiritual transformation:

- From impulsive to thoughtful
- From fearful to courageous
- From denial to martyrdom
- From student to teacher
- From weak reed to solid rock

### Theological Significance

Peter's life illustrates key Christian principles:

- Divine grace transforms human weakness
- Failure isn't final in God's kingdom
- Leadership emerges through humble service
- Bold confession must match faithful living
- Restoration is possible after spiritual failure

### Contemporary Relevance

Peter's story continues to resonate because he:

- Models authentic discipleship with real struggles
- Demonstrates the power of divine grace
- Shows how God uses flawed people
- Exemplifies genuine repentance and restoration
- Proves that failure isn't fatal to spiritual growth

### Legacy Impact

Peter's influence extends through:

- His role in church foundation
- His biblical writings
- His example of leadership
- His pattern of restoration
- His model of transformation

Peter's journey from impetuous fisherman to foundational apostle continues to inspire believers worldwide, offering hope that God can use anyone who submits to divine transformation.

## Andrew: Apostle Who Was The First Disciple of Jesus

### Origins and Background

Who was the first disciple of Jesus? Andrew, like his brother Simon Peter, was born in the fishing village of Bethsaida on the Sea of Galilee. As a fisherman by trade, he worked alongside his brother in their family business. Before following Jesus, Andrew was a disciple of John the Baptist, demonstrating his early spiritual seeking nature. His Greek name suggests possible cultural connections to the broader Hellenistic world, which may have influenced his later ministry.

### The Call to Discipleship

Andrew holds the distinction of being the first disciple of Jesus, earning him the title "Protokletos" (First-Called) in Orthodox tradition. After hearing John the Baptist declare Jesus as the "Lamb of God," Andrew spent a day with Jesus and became convinced of His messianic identity. His immediate response was to find his brother Simon Peter and bring him to Jesus, marking his characteristic role as one who brings others to Christ.

Role in Jesus' Ministry

Throughout the Gospels, Andrew appears in several key moments:

- Brought Peter to Jesus, initiating his brother's discipleship
- Identified the boy with five loaves and two fish before feeding of 5,000
- Served as bridge between Greek seekers and Jesus
- Part of the inner group who asked about the signs of the end times
- Present during many key events in Jesus' ministry
- Often worked behind the scenes in supporting roles

### Leadership in the Early Church

While less prominent than his brother in Acts, Andrew's influence continued:

1. Early Church Development

- Present at Pentecost
- Participated in early church expansion
- Supported apostolic mission work

2. Ministry Focus

- Known for personal evangelism
- Emphasized bringing individuals to Jesus
- Built bridges between cultures

3. Cultural Impact

- Connected Greek and Jewish believers
- Facilitated cross-cultural ministry
- Expanded church's cultural reach

### Biblical Writings

While Andrew didn't author any biblical books, his influence appears in:

- John's Gospel, where his actions are specifically highlighted
- Early church traditions and accounts
- Testimonies preserved by other apostles

### Traditional Impact and Legacy

Church tradition records that Andrew:

- Ministered in Scythia (modern Ukraine/Russia)
- Preached throughout Greece and Asia Minor
- Founded the church in Byzantium (later Constantinople)
- Was martyred in Patras, Greece on an X-shaped cross
- Became patron saint of Scotland, Russia, and Greece
- Influenced Eastern Orthodox Christianity significantly

### Character Analysis

Andrew's personality reveals distinct characteristics:

Strengths

- Eager to share good news
- People-oriented connector
- Humble service mindset
- Cross-cultural sensitivity
- Personal evangelism focus
- Problem-solving abilities

### Growth Areas

- Content with secondary role
- Sometimes overshadowed by Peter
- Worked quietly in background
- Less publicly prominent
- Focused on individuals over crowds

### Transformation

Andrew's journey shows significant development:

- From seeker to finder
- From fisherman to soul-winner
- From follower to leader
- From local fisherman to global evangelist
- From cultural insider to cross-cultural missionary

### Theological Significance

Andrew's life illustrates important principles:

- The power of personal evangelism
- Value of behind-the-scenes ministry
- Importance of bringing others to Jesus
- Significance of cultural bridge-building
- Beauty of humble service

### Contemporary Relevance

Andrew's example continues to inspire through:

- Personal evangelism methods

### Legacy Impact

Andrew's influence continues through:

- Model of humble service

Andrew's legacy as the "First-Called" and his consistent pattern of bringing others to Jesus offers a compelling model of discipleship that emphasizes personal evangelism and humble service. His life demonstrates how quiet faithfulness and personal connections can have far-reaching impact in God's kingdom.

## James son of Zebedee: The First Martyred Apostle

### Origins and Background

James, son of Zebedee and Salome, was born into a relatively prosperous fishing family in Galilee. Along with his younger brother John, he worked in the family fishing business, which was successful enough to employ hired servants. His mother Salome was likely a follower of Jesus and may have been Jesus' mother's sister, potentially making James a cousin of Jesus. The family's business partnership with Peter and Andrew suggests they held some standing in the local fishing community.

### The Call to Discipleship

Jesus called James and John while they were mending nets with their father Zebedee. Their immediate response—leaving their father and the family business—demonstrated remarkable commitment. The brothers' fierce dedication and passionate nature soon earned them the nickname "Boanerges" (Sons of Thunder) from Jesus, reflecting their intense personalities and zeal for Jesus' mission.

Role in Jesus' Ministry

James held a privileged position as part of Jesus' inner circle:

- Present at the raising of Jairus' daughter
- Witnessed the Transfiguration
- Present during Jesus' agony in Gethsemane
- One of only three disciples to witness these pivotal events
- Demonstrated special closeness to Jesus throughout ministry
- Participated in private teachings and moments

### Leadership in the Early Church

James' leadership was brief but significant:

1. Early Church Development

- Active in Jerusalem church formation
- Present at Pentecost
- Helped establish early Christian community
- Served until martyrdom under Herod Agrippa I

2. Martyrdom Impact

- First apostle to be martyred (Acts 12:2)
- Death sparked increased persecution
- Demonstrated cost of discipleship
- Inspired early church persistence

3. Leadership Legacy

- Set pattern of apostolic martyrdom
- Showed courage under persecution
- Inspired faithfulness unto death

### Biblical Writings

While James didn't author any biblical books, his influence appears in:

- Gospel accounts of his experiences
- Acts' record of his martyrdom
- His brother John's writings
- Early church historical accounts

### Traditional Impact and Legacy

Church tradition holds that James:

- Preached in Spain before his death
- Became patron saint of Spain and pilgrims
- Inspired the Camino de Santiago pilgrimage
- His remains allegedly rest in Santiago de Compostela
- Influenced Spanish Christianity significantly
- Inspired countless pilgrims through centuries

### Transformation

James' relatively brief ministry and martyrdom provide a powerful testimony of complete dedication to Christ. His transformation from an ambitious "Son of Thunder" to a faithful martyr demonstrates how divine grace can redirect natural passion and zeal into powerful service for God's kingdom. ## John: The Beloved Disciple

### Origins and Background

John, the younger son of Zebedee and Salome, grew up in a prosperous fishing family on the shores of Galilee. Working alongside his brother James in the family business, he was likely the youngest of all the disciples. His family's means and connections are evidenced by their hired servants and his later personal acquaintance with the high priest. Like his brother, he may have been Jesus' cousin through his mother Salome's relationship to Mary.

### The Call to Discipleship

Called simultaneously with his brother James, John left their father's fishing business immediately to follow Jesus. Originally a disciple of John the Baptist, he likely witnessed Jesus' baptism. His youth and passionate nature contributed to the nickname "Son of Thunder," shared with his brother James, though this fiery temperament would be transformed through his relationship with Jesus into deep love and contemplative wisdom.

Role in Jesus' Ministry

## Philip: The Practical Seeker

### Origins and Background

Philip hailed from Bethsaida, the same town as Andrew and Peter, suggesting possible prior acquaintance with these fellow disciples. His Greek name indicates possible Hellenistic influence in his family or background. This cultural bridge position may explain his later role as an intermediary between Greek seekers and Jesus. Coming from a town known for its fishing industry and mixed cultural heritage, Philip likely had exposure to both Jewish and Gentile perspectives from an early age.

### The Call to Discipleship

Philip was personally called by Jesus with the simple command "Follow me." His immediate response was to find Nathanael (Bartholomew) and share his discovery of the Messiah, demonstrating an early pattern of both seeking and sharing truth. His analytical nature showed itself even in this first encounter, as he responded to Nathanael's skepticism with the practical invitation to "Come and see."

### Are the 12 Disciples and Apostles the Same?

The terms "disciple" and "apostle" are often used interchangeably, but they are not the same.

A disciple is someone who follows and learns from a teacher, while an apostle is someone who is sent out on a mission. In the case of Jesus, the 12 disciples were his students or learners, while the apostles were those He commissioned to go out and spread His message.

The apostles were chosen from among the disciples, but not all disciples were apostles.

The 12 Disciples were handpicked by Jesus himself, while the apostles were a select group within that larger group of disciples. Judas Iscariot was among the 12 disciples, but he ultimately betrayed Jesus and was replaced by Matthias as the 12th apostle.

#### More Apostles in the New Testament

In addition to the 12 apostles, there were other individuals who were referred to as apostles in the New Testament, such as Paul, Barnabas, and James (the brother of Jesus). These individuals were not among the original 12, but they were still recognized as apostles due to their important roles in spreading the message of Jesus Christ.

Ultimately, the distinction between disciples and apostles is not as important as the role that each played in spreading the message of Jesus.

Both groups were crucial in establishing the early Christian church and laying the foundation for the faith that billions of people around the world practice today.

By studying their stories and following their example, we can continue their legacy and spread the message of Jesus Christ to all those who need to hear it.

### Disciple-Making Movements Today

The model of disciples making disciple has its roots in the way Jesus trained and sent out his 12 disciples to spread the good news of the gospel. Today, a term for this idea called "disciple making movements" is a concept that has had a profound impact on modern-day evangelism and understanding of missions.

Instead of solely focusing on individual conversions, disciple making movements prioritize the training and multiplication of disciples who can then go on to make more disciples. This approach is rooted in Jesus’ own model of ministry, where he chose 12 disciples to follow him closely and trained them to continue his mission after he was gone.

The 12 disciples of Jesus were not just passive recipients of Jesus’ teachings; they were active participants in the spread of the gospel.

They traveled with Jesus, learned from him, and later spread his message to others. They were sent out two by two to proclaim the good news and heal the sick (Mark 6:7). Jesus' focus on making disciples, rather than simply acquiring followers, has influenced modern evangelism by encouraging a deeper emphasis on training and equipping believers to share the gospel with others.

The concept of disciple making movements has gained popularity in recent years with many missions organizations championing the approach.

#### The Fruit of Disciple Making Movements

By focusing on multiplying disciples, rather than just adding converts, disciple making movements have seen significant growth in places where traditional evangelistic methods have struggled. This approach has proven especially effective in reaching people groups who are resistant to traditional evangelistic methods.

In Summary, the 12 disciples of Jesus serve as an example of the power of disciple making movements in spreading the gospel message. By following Jesus’ model of ministry, we can train and equip believers to become disciple makers, who in turn can train and equip others, leading to a multiplication of disciples and the growth of the church.
