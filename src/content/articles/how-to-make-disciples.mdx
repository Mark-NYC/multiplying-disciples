<ArticleIntro>To make disciples is to help someone follow Jesus and help others do the same, by walking with them through a simple, repeatable rhythm: hear from God's Word, obey what you hear, and pass it on to someone else. It is less a program you run than a life you share with one person at a time.</ArticleIntro>

Jesus gave the whole assignment in a single sentence: "go and make disciples of all nations... teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you" (Matthew 28:19-20). Notice the verb. Not *teach them everything*, but teach them to *obey* everything. A disciple is not someone who knows more. A disciple is someone who does what Jesus said and helps the next person do the same.

That is good news for you, because it means you don't need a seminary degree, a title, or a curriculum to start. You need to be following Jesus yourself, one person who isn't yet, and a simple pattern you can repeat and hand off.

Here is that pattern, and how to begin it this week.

## The pattern in one picture

Almost every healthy disciple-making relationship runs the same loop, over and over, in an ordinary setting.

<InsightCard label="The Disciple-Making Loop">
**Look back:** How are you doing? What did you do with what we read last time?

**Look up:** Read a short passage of Scripture together. Ask what it says about God, and what it says about people.

**Look forward:** Decide one thing to obey this week, and name one person to tell about it.
</InsightCard>

That's it. You are not lecturing; you are reading, obeying, and multiplying alongside someone. The [Three Thirds meeting format](/three-thirds/) builds this loop into a single hour, and it's simple enough that the person you disciple can lead it for someone else next month. Reproducibility is the whole point. If what you do can't be copied by a brand-new believer, it will add disciples but never multiply them.

## Start by following Jesus yourself

You cannot give away what you don't have. Before you disciple anyone, the first question is whether you are living the thing you'll be asking them to live.

This isn't a call to be an expert. It's a call to be a genuine follower, someone with a real (if imperfect) rhythm of hearing God in Scripture and obeying it. The [three core habits of a disciple](/the-3-core-habits-of-a-disciple/) are a good self-check: are you abiding in Christ daily, praying for people who don't know him, and able to share the gospel simply? If those are wobbly, start there. You'll be discipling out of your own obedience, not ahead of it.

<ScriptureBlock reference="John 8:31">
To the Jews who had believed him, Jesus said, "If you hold to my teaching, you are really my disciples."
</ScriptureBlock>

## Pray for and pick one person

Making disciples starts with a name, not a crowd. Ask God to show you one person, then look at the people already around you: family, coworkers, neighbors, friends. Jesus told his followers to pray for the harvest before they went into it (Luke 10:2), so pray first.

Watch for what the Bible calls a person of peace: someone who welcomes you, is open to spiritual conversation, and tends to draw others in. You are not trying to convince everyone. You are looking for the one God has already prepared. When you find an open person, invest there.

If the person isn't yet following Jesus, your first step isn't a Bible study. It's a conversation. Your own story is the most natural on-ramp there is, so if you've never put words to it, start with [how to share your testimony](/how-to-share-your-testimony/), and keep a simple gospel tool like [the 3 Circles](/the-three-circles-gospel-presentation-step-by-step/) ready for when they're curious.

## Meet simply, and meet regularly

Discipleship happens in the ordinary rhythm of showing up. Pick a time and a place, a coffee shop, a kitchen table, a park bench, and meet.

Keep the meeting simple enough to reproduce. Read a short passage. Ask three questions: What does this say about God? What does it say about us? What will you obey this week? Then pray, and go. You are modeling something the other person could lead themselves within a few weeks, which is exactly what you want.

The engine that keeps it moving is gentle, honest follow-up. Next time you meet, you ask about last week's obedience step, not to shame anyone, but because [friendly accountability](/unlock-the-power-of-friendly-accountability/) is what turns good intentions into a changed life. Grace-filled, specific, expected questions ("How did that conversation with your brother go?") do more than any amount of new information.

## Obey together, then hand it off

Here is the difference between making disciples and merely gathering an audience: you are aiming from day one for the person you disciple to disciple someone else.

Paul named this out loud when he trained Timothy:

<ScriptureBlock reference="2 Timothy 2:2">
And the things you have heard me say in the presence of many witnesses entrust to reliable people who will also be qualified to teach others.
</ScriptureBlock>

Count the generations in that one verse: Paul, to Timothy, to reliable people, to others. Four links in the chain. That is multiplication, not addition, and it's the design. So from the beginning, when your disciple obeys something, ask the follow-on question: "Who are you going to tell?" When they're ready, don't just keep meeting with them. Help them start meeting with someone new, and coach them from the side.

<InsightCard label="Addition vs. Multiplication">
**Addition** asks, "How many people can I personally disciple?" The answer is always small.

**Multiplication** asks, "How many disciple-makers can I help launch?" The answer compounds, because each one launches others.
</InsightCard>

## How Jesus made disciples

If the pattern above sounds familiar, it's because it's simply what Jesus did. He didn't open a school. He called ordinary people to follow him, lived life with them, let them watch him, sent them to try it, debriefed what happened, and then handed them the mission.

"Come, follow me," he said, "and I will send you out to fish for people" (Mark 1:17). Following and fishing were joined from the first day. He modeled ministry while they watched, assisted him, then went out on their own while he coached, and finally he left them to do it and to teach others. That progression, watching before doing and doing before leading, is the same one you'll walk your disciple through. It's worth studying the whole arc in [the way Jesus made disciples through the Four Fields](/four-fields-of-kingdom-growth/), because his method is still the clearest one there is.

## The mistakes that quietly stall it

Most disciple-making doesn't fail loudly. It drifts into one of a few comfortable dead ends.

<WarningCard label="Where It Stalls">
**Information instead of obedience.** You keep meeting, keep reading, keep learning, and nothing changes, because no one is actually asked to do anything. Knowledge grows; the disciple doesn't.

**A program instead of a relationship.** You launch a class or a curriculum and mistake attendance for discipleship. People can complete a course and never learn to follow Jesus.

**Addition instead of multiplication.** You disciple people well but never help them disciple anyone, so the chain stops with you.
</WarningCard>

The fix for all three is the same: keep asking the two questions that force obedience and multiplication. *What will you obey?* and *Who will you tell?* Everything healthy flows from there.

## Take the first step this week

You don't make disciples by finishing an article about it. You make disciples by starting with a person.

<PracticeCard label="Your Next Step">
1. **Name one person.** Write down the name of one person in your life who isn't following Jesus, or is new to it and needs help growing.
2. **Pray for them by name** this week, and ask God for an open door.
3. **Take one step toward them.** Invite them to coffee, share a piece of your story, or ask if they'd read a short passage of the Bible with you.
4. **Decide your own obedience.** Name one thing you'll do this week because of what you've read here, and tell someone you did it.
</PracticeCard>

Start small, start with one, and start this week. The person God wants to reach through you is almost certainly already in your life.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does it mean to make disciples?

To make disciples is to help someone become a follower of Jesus and grow into a follower who can help others follow him too. It involves the whole arc: sharing the gospel, helping a person trust and obey Jesus, and walking with them until they can disciple someone else. It is not the same as making converts. A convert has made a decision; a disciple is learning to obey everything Jesus commanded (Matthew 28:20).

### How do I start discipling someone?

Start with one person you already know, meet with them regularly in a simple setting, and build every meeting around reading a short passage, deciding what to obey, and naming who to tell. Keep it simple enough that they could lead it themselves. You are modeling a life, not delivering a course.

### Do I need to be trained or qualified to make disciples?

No. You need to be genuinely following Jesus yourself and willing to share what you're learning. Jesus made disciples out of fishermen and tax collectors. Training helps, and it's worth getting, but the qualification is obedience, not expertise. A witness only has to tell what he has seen.

### How long does it take to make a disciple?

There's no fixed timeline, because you're aiming at a changed life, not a completed syllabus. Some habits form in weeks; maturity takes years. The goal isn't a graduation date. It's a disciple who is following Jesus and has begun helping someone else do the same.
