Someone just started following Jesus. What you do in the next month matters more than almost anything that comes after, because this is when their habits get set. The temptation is to hand them a book or a class and check back later. Do not. The first 30 days are for building a lifestyle, and a lifestyle is caught more than it is taught.

## The goal of the first 30 days

You are not trying to get information into a new believer. You are trying to help them start living the way a follower of Jesus lives: following him daily and fishing for people. That identity comes straight from Jesus' first words to his own disciples.

<ScriptureBlock reference="Mark 1:17">
"Come, follow me," Jesus said, "and I will send you out to fish for people."
</ScriptureBlock>

Following and fishing. That is the life you are handing on, and 30 days is about how long it takes for a new rhythm to start feeling normal. So spend the time where it counts: with them. Jesus said the harvest is plentiful and the workers are few (Luke 10:2), and the fastest way to add a worker is to walk beside a new one in the field until they can do it without you.

## Teach them to feed themselves with SOS

The first thing a new believer needs is to hear from God on their own, not just through you. Otherwise they stay dependent, and dependence does not reproduce. So teach them a simple way to feed themselves from Scripture called SOS: Say, Obey, Share.

<InsightCard label="SOS: how to feed yourself from Scripture">
**Say.** What does this passage actually say? Read it, and put it in your own words.

**Obey.** What is God asking me to do about it? Name one thing to obey this week.

**Share.** Who will I tell what I found?
</InsightCard>

Do it by reading the book of Mark together, a chapter or a story at a time. Mark is short, fast, and full of Jesus in action, which makes it a good first gospel. And do not just quiz them. Share your own SOS too. When you tell them what you are going to obey and who you are going to share it with, they learn that this is how everyone follows Jesus, not a beginner exercise you have outgrown. It keeps the whole thing honest, because Scripture was always meant to be obeyed, not just discussed.

<ScriptureBlock reference="James 1:22">
Do not merely listen to the word, and so deceive yourselves. Do what it says.
</ScriptureBlock>

## Turn the tools into rhythms: invite them into your life

A new believer does not need more tools handed to them. They need to see the tools lived. So invite them into your habits and rhythms. Let them come with you when you pray for lost friends, map an oikos, share your story, or open a Bible with someone. The [4-1-1 tool](https://obey.tools/4-questions.html) they started with is not a one-time worksheet. It becomes a way of life when they watch you live it and then do it alongside you.

This is why the first 30 days take time and not just meetings. You are not scheduling a class. You are letting someone shadow your walk with Jesus until your normal becomes their normal.

## Build the 3-2-1 rhythm

Habits need a shape. Call a new believer to a simple, consistent weekly rhythm that builds following, fishing, and family all at once. Keep it in front of them as 3-2-1.

<InsightCard label="The 3-2-1 weekly rhythm">
**3: Following and fishing (about 30 minutes a day, roughly 3 hours a week).** Daily time with Jesus in the Word and prayer, and eyes open for people to reach.

**2: Gathering (about 2 hours a week).** A [three-thirds](/three-thirds/) reproducing gathering with other believers, built to multiply rather than just meet.

**1: Accountability (1 hour a week).** Once they show themselves to be FAT, faithful, available, and teachable, invite them into a weekly accountability or team meeting.
</InsightCard>

Notice that the 1 is earned, not assumed. The weekly accountability or team meeting is for those who are proving faithful with the little they have been given, available to be interrupted, and teachable when they are corrected. You do not withhold it to be exclusive. You wait for it because accountability only works with someone who actually wants to grow.

## The curriculum is your life

Here is the whole thing in one sentence: the goal of the first 30 days is time with them, in your harvest field and in theirs, until they adopt the habits and rhythms of following and fishing.

Take them into your world. Let them watch you love your family, handle a hard week, share your faith at work, and open Scripture in the morning. Then go into their world. Meet their friends, walk their street, and help them reach the people God has already put around them. You are not building a program. You are helping a new believer trade their old lifestyle for the life of a disciple, and that only happens up close.

<PracticeCard>
This week, pick one new or young believer and do three things: read one chapter of Mark with them and both share your SOS, invite them into one thing you were already going to do (pray, share, gather), and put the 3-2-1 rhythm in front of them as the shape to aim for. Then get on the calendar for next week.
</PracticeCard>

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I follow up with a new believer?

Spend real time with them, and build habits rather than deliver information. In practice that means teaching them to feed themselves from Scripture with SOS (reading the book of Mark together), inviting them into your own rhythms of prayer, fishing, and gathering, and calling them to a consistent weekly 3-2-1 rhythm. The aim of the first month is not a completed course. It is a new believer who is starting to live the way a follower of Jesus lives.

### What is SOS Bible reading?

SOS stands for Say, Obey, Share. You read a passage and ask three questions: what does it say, what will I obey, and who will I share it with. It is a simple, reproducible way to feed yourself from Scripture that keeps the focus on obedience, not just information. Teach it to a new believer by doing it together in the book of Mark, and by sharing your own Say, Obey, and Share alongside theirs.

### What is the 3-2-1 rhythm?

It is a weekly rhythm for a growing disciple: 3 hours of following and fishing (about 30 minutes a day of time with Jesus and reaching people), 2 hours gathering in a three-thirds reproducing group, and 1 hour of accountability or a team meeting once the person is faithful, available, and teachable. It builds daily devotion, church life, and accountability into a shape simple enough to remember and reproduce.

## Where to go next

New believer follow-up is one stretch of the larger path in [one-on-one discipleship](/one-on-one-discipleship/) and [how to make disciples](/how-to-make-disciples/). As a new believer grows steady in these rhythms, the next step is helping them do it with someone else, which is what [training disciples](/how-to-train-disciples/) is for. Start this week: one chapter of Mark, one shared SOS, and one rhythm to aim at.
