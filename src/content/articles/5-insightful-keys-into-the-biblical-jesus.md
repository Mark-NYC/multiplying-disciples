Christianity is the largest religion in the world, with over 2 billion followers, and the teachings of Jesus Christ are at the heart of this faith.

Whether you are asking questions or thinking through how to explain your faith to others, this post will be helpful. Let's dive into 5 insightful keys into the Biblical Jesus.

### What Are The Main Teachings of the New Testament?

**The New Testament is filled with the teachings of Jesus Christ, but three key themes stand out: loving God, loving people, and making disciples.**

Loving God is the cornerstone of Jesus' teachings.

He emphasized the importance of having a personal relationship with God and worshipping Him in spirit and truth. In Matthew 22:37-38, Jesus says, "Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind. This is the first and greatest commandment." Jesus' teachings show that loving God is not just about following rules or attending religious ceremonies, but about having a genuine, heartfelt connection with Him.

Loving people is another central theme in Jesus' teachings.

He showed love and compassion to everyone he met, regardless of their social status, ethnicity, or background. In Matthew 22:39, Jesus says, "And the second is like it: 'Love your neighbor as yourself.'" This means treating others with the same kindness and respect that we would want for ourselves.

Jesus also taught that we should show love to our enemies and pray for those who persecute us (Matthew 5:44).

Finally, making disciples is a critical part of Jesus' teachings.

In Matthew 28:19-20, Jesus commands his followers, "Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you." Jesus wants his followers to share the good news of his teachings with others and help them grow in their relationship with God.

Disciple-making involves not only sharing the Gospel but also teaching new believers to obey God's commands and live according to His will.

These teachings are just as relevant and vital today as they were over 2,000 years ago. By living out these commands, we can transform our lives and the lives of those around us, making a positive impact on the world.

### What Did Jesus Preach About the Most?

Jesus preached about many things during his time on earth, but perhaps the most important and consistent theme throughout his teachings was the kingdom of God. This concept is mentioned over 100 times in the New Testament and refers to God's reign and rule over all creation.

In his teachings, Jesus described the kingdom of God as being both a present reality and a future hope.

He often used parables to explain what the kingdom was like and to help his listeners understand its significance.

One of the key messages Jesus preached about the kingdom was that it was available to everyone, regardless of their social status or background. He demonstrated this by regularly associating with and showing compassion to those who were marginalized or considered outcasts in society.

Jesus also emphasized the importance of repentance and faith as necessary components of entering into the kingdom of God. He called on people to turn away from their sins and to trust in God's forgiveness and grace.

Another important aspect of Jesus's teaching on the kingdom of God was the idea of living out its values in our daily lives. This included things like loving our neighbors, caring for the poor and oppressed, and seeking justice and peace in the world.

Ultimately, Jesus's preaching about the kingdom of God was a call to live in a way that honored and glorified God, both individually and as a community. It was a message of hope and transformation, of the restoration of all things to their original intended purpose.

### What Are The Three Most Important Teachings of Christianity?

Christianity is centered around several key beliefs, but three teachings stand out as the most important.

These three teachings are foundational to the Christian faith and shape the way that Christians understand God, salvation, and the purpose of human existence.

**First and foremost, the Trinity is a fundamental teaching of Christianity.**

This belief holds that there is one God who exists in three distinct persons: the Father, the Son (Jesus Christ), and the Holy Spirit. This belief has been central to Christian theology since the earliest days of the church and is a defining characteristic of Christian faith.

**The second most important teaching of Christianity is salvation through faith in Jesus Christ.**

Christians believe that human beings are sinful and separated from God, but that Jesus Christ's death and resurrection provide a way for people to be saved from their sins and receive eternal life. Christians place their faith in Jesus as their Lord and Savior, trusting that through his sacrifice, they have been reconciled to God.

**Finally, the third most important teaching of Christianity is the resurrection of Jesus Christ.**

According to the New Testament, Jesus was crucified and buried, but on the third day, he rose from the dead. This event is central to the Christian faith because it demonstrates Jesus' power over death and his ability to offer eternal life to all who believe in him.

Taken together, these three teachings form the bedrock of the Christian faith. They help Christians to understand God's nature, the nature of human beings, and the nature of salvation. Christians believe that by accepting these teachings and placing their faith in Jesus Christ, they can have a relationship with God and receive eternal life.

### What are Jesus' Two Ways of Teaching?

Jesus was a master teacher, and he used various methods to convey his message. Two of his most significant ways of teaching were through asking questions and through modeling or "withness."

One of Jesus' most common teaching methods was to ask questions.

He often used questions to challenge his listeners and to help them think more deeply about their faith and their actions. Through questions, Jesus encouraged people to reflect on their own beliefs and to engage with him in a dialogue.

He asked questions to provoke thought, to test understanding, and to encourage deeper reflection. For example, in Matthew 16:13-16, Jesus asks his disciples, "Who do people say that the Son of Man is?" and then follows up with "But who do you say that I am?"

This line of questioning forces the disciples to reflect on their own beliefs and articulate them to Jesus.

Another way Jesus taught was through modeling or "withness."

Jesus didn't just preach about how to live a godly life; he lived it. He spent time with people, showing them through his own actions what it looked like to live a life centered on God. Jesus walked alongside his disciples, demonstrating what it meant to love God and love others.

Through his actions, he showed them the way to live out their faith. In John 13:15, Jesus says, "For I have given you an example, that you should also do as I have done to you."

By using these two methods of teaching, Jesus was able to effectively communicate his message and bring about change in the lives of his followers. He didn't just tell people what to do; he showed them through his own life and asked them questions that helped them understand their own beliefs and actions.

Through his use of questioning and modeling, Jesus was able to connect with his audience on a deeper level and inspire them to live out their faith.

### What Was The Reason Jesus Came to Earth?

Jesus came to earth to save people from their sins and reconcile them to God. He accomplished this by dying on the cross and rising from the dead. His death and resurrection provide the way for people to have eternal life and be reconciled to God.
