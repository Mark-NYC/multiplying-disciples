Ask ten people what makes a church a church and you will get ten answers about buildings, denominations, ordained pastors, a certain style of service, or a minimum crowd. None of those is what Scripture points to. The Bible does not define the church by its address or its size. It defines the church by who it belongs to and what it does together, and the whole thing is simple enough to count on one hand.

## The five W's of a church

We call it a handy guide to church, because it fits on your hand: five fingers, five questions, one picture of what a church is. It comes straight from the birth of the church in Acts 2, so read the anchor passage first.

<ScriptureBlock reference="Acts 2:41-42">
Those who accepted his message were baptized, and about three thousand were added to their number that day. They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer.
</ScriptureBlock>

Now count it out. Who, What, Where, When, and Why. When those five are present, you have a church, no matter how ordinary the room.

<InsightCard label="The five W's of a church (Acts 2:36-47)">
**Who?** Baptized, obedient believers who have turned to Jesus. On the first day, about 3,000 repented and were baptized (Acts 2:41). Jesus called this group a family: whoever does the will of the Father is his brother and sister (Matthew 12:50). Scripture pictures them as a Bride, a Body, and a Temple.

**What?** The practices of a healthy church. There are twelve of them in the [Church Waffle](/movement-resources/12-practice-church-circle/), things like the Word, prayer, the Lord's Supper, giving, worship, and mission. You do not need all twelve at full strength on day one. Living out a few creates a culture where the others start to grow.

**Where?** House to house, and in public. Most churches in the New Testament met in homes (Acts 2:46, Romans 16:5), and also gathered publicly. A church needs people and Jesus, not a dedicated building.

**When?** Not just a weekly event, but a daily, relational rhythm. Acts 2 shows them meeting "every day," in the temple courts and from house to house. Church was a culture they lived, not a service they attended, and Scripture tells us not to give up meeting together (Hebrews 10:24-25).

**Why?** To host God's presence and do everything for his glory (1 Corinthians 10:31). Underneath every practice, this is the point: a family gathered around Jesus, for God.
</InsightCard>

That is a church, whether it happens in a temple courtyard or a living room. Notice what is on your hand and what is not. Baptized believers, the practices, homes, a daily rhythm, and the glory of God are all here. A building, a budget, a stage, and a paid staff are not.

This guide is really the first of a pair. Think of it as the left hand, the picture of a church start. There is also a right hand, a handy guide to church health, for a church growing toward maturity. And between the two hands is the messy middle, the real and often bumpy stretch where a young gathering is becoming a healthy church. This article is about the left hand: what makes a gathering a church in the first place.

## What a church does not require

Because we grew up picturing church as a place, it helps to say plainly what a church does not need in order to be one.

<ScriptureBlock reference="Matthew 18:20">
For where two or three gather in my name, there am I with them.
</ScriptureBlock>

A church does not require a dedicated building; the New Testament church met in homes. It does not require a large crowd; Jesus set the floor at two or three gathered in his name. It does not require a professional clergyman to be valid; it requires Jesus, present among his people, and a family willing to obey him. When the five W's are present, you have a church, no matter how small or how ordinary the room. When they are absent, adding a building and a budget will not create one.

## When a gathering is not yet a church

The five W's also help you see honestly when a gathering has not become a church yet. A Bible study can meet faithfully every week and still be missing several of them: no baptized believers who confess Jesus as Lord (the Who), none of the practices beyond discussion (the What), no shared life beyond the meeting (the Where and When). That is not a criticism. A discovery Bible study full of seeking people is doing exactly what it should. It simply has not become a church yet.

The honest question is not "do we meet?" but "which of the five W's are actually true of us?" That is worth naming plainly, because a group that knows it is not yet a church can grow into one on purpose. This is the messy middle, and knowing where the line is helps you see [when a discipleship group becomes a church](/when-a-discipleship-group-becomes-a-church/).

## Grace, not a scorecard

One caution. The five W's are a description of a church, not a test you pass to earn the title. A young simple church may be weak in prayer or just learning to share the Lord's Supper, and it is no less a church for being immature. Acts 2 is not a bar to clear before God will call you his people. It is a picture of what his people look like, so you can see what is already alive among you and what to grow next. The goal is a healthy family, not a perfect score, and growing that health is what the right hand of the guide is for.

<PracticeCard>
Hold up your hand and walk the five W's over your own gathering. Who: are there baptized believers who confess Jesus as Lord? What: which of the practices are alive among you? Where and When: is there shared life beyond the meeting, or only the meeting? Why: is it all aimed at God's presence and glory? Name the one W that is weakest, and take one concrete step to grow it this month.
</PracticeCard>

## Frequently asked questions

### What makes a church a church?

A church is a family of believers, described in Acts 2:36-47 by five simple questions you can count on one hand. Who: baptized, obedient believers who have turned to Jesus and are a family that does the Father's will. What: the practices of a healthy church, twelve of them in the Church Waffle. Where: house to house and in public, not a required building. When: a daily, relational rhythm, not only a weekly event. Why: to host God's presence and live for his glory. Where those five are present, even among two or three gathered in Jesus' name, there is a church.

### Is a Bible study a church?

Not necessarily. A Bible study can meet faithfully and still be missing several of the five W's: baptized believers who confess Jesus as Lord, the practices of a church beyond discussion, and shared life beyond the meeting itself. A discovery Bible study full of seeking people is doing good and necessary work, but it is not yet a church. It can grow into one as people come to faith, are baptized, commit to one another, and begin living out the full pattern of Acts 2.

### Does a church need a building or a pastor?

No. The first churches met in homes and were often led by ordinary believers, not professional clergy. Jesus said that where two or three gather in his name, he is present (Matthew 18:20), which means a church needs Jesus and a family willing to obey him far more than it needs a building or a paid pastor. Buildings and leaders can serve a church, but they do not create one, and their absence does not disqualify one.
