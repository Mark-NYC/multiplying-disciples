That is the short answer. The longer one is worth having, because "what did Jesus command?" is a bigger question than people expect, and the way he answered it himself is the key to the whole thing.

## He Gave a Lot of Commands

Open the Gospels and you find Jesus commanding all kinds of things. Love your enemies. Do not worry. Forgive, and keep forgiving. Do not judge. Pray in secret. Give to the needy. Let your light shine. Do not store up treasure on earth. Deny yourself and follow him. Go and make disciples. People who set out to list them all end up with dozens, because Jesus was constantly telling his followers not just what to believe, but what to do.

So there is no tidy set of ten. His teaching is full of instructions for how a follower actually lives. Which raises the obvious question: if there are that many, where do you even start? Jesus answered that himself.

## He Summed Them Up in Love

When someone asked Jesus which command was the greatest, he did not hesitate, and he did not stop at one:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Matthew 22:37-40">
Jesus replied: "Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind." This is the first and greatest commandment. And the second is like it: "Love your neighbor as yourself." All the Law and the Prophets hang on these two commandments.
</ScriptureBlock>

Everything hangs on those two. Love God, love your neighbor. Every other command is one of those two worked out in a specific situation. Forgiving your brother is loving your neighbor. Praying is loving God. If you want one thing to organize all of Jesus' commands, it is love.

Then, on the night before he died, he added a command he called new:

<ScriptureBlock reference="John 13:34-35">
A new command I give you: Love one another. As I have loved you, so you must love one another. By this everyone will know that you are my disciples, if you love one another.
</ScriptureBlock>

Love one another as he loved us. This is the family mark of his followers, the thing the watching world would notice. So the center of everything Jesus commanded is love: for God, for neighbor, and for one another.

<PullQuote>Every command Jesus gave is one of two things worked out in a real situation: love God, or love your neighbor.</PullQuote>

## The Command That Carries All the Others

There is one more command that gathers up the rest, and it is the one Jesus left his followers with at the very end:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Matthew 28:19-20">
Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you.
</ScriptureBlock>

Read that last phrase again: teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you. Jesus expected his commands to be passed on and obeyed, from one follower to the next, in every nation. His commands were never meant to sit in a book. They were meant to be lived and handed on. That is the whole reason he [made disciples](/how-jesus-made-disciples/) the way he did.

## He Meant Them to Be Obeyed

This is the part that changes how you read all of it. Jesus did not give his commands as ideals to admire. He tied loving him directly to obeying him:

<ScriptureBlock reference="John 14:15">
If you love me, keep my commands.
</ScriptureBlock>

And he was blunt with people who called him Lord but did not do what he said: "Why do you call me, 'Lord, Lord,' and do not do what I say?" (Luke 6:46). For Jesus, obedience was not the opposite of love or grace. It was the proof of love and the path of following. You do not obey to earn his love. You obey because you have it, and because you trust that the one who commands you is good.

<InsightCard label="What Jesus commanded, in short">
Many commands, all summed up in love: love God, love your neighbor, love one another as he loved you. And one command that carries them all: make disciples and teach them to obey everything he commanded. His commands are meant to be obeyed and passed on, not just known.
</InsightCard>

## Where to Start

You cannot obey "everything Jesus commanded" all at once, and you are not meant to. The way disciples have always started is by taking a few of his clearest, most basic commands and beginning to obey them, one at a time. Disciple-makers often gather about a dozen of these first commands and practices, each tied to a Bible story, into a simple starter set called the [commands of Christ](/what-are-the-commands-of-christ/). It is a good place to begin: repent and believe, be baptized, love one another, pray, give, gather, go and make disciples, and more.

Pick one you know you have been leaving undone, read the story behind it, and obey it this week. Then help someone else do the same. If you are helping others start, [commands of Christ training](/commands-of-christ-training/) walks a group through all twelve as a way of learning to follow Jesus together.

<WarningCard label="Obey from love, not fear">
It is easy to hear "keep my commands" as a heavy list of rules you have to get right to stay in God's good graces. That is not what Jesus meant. He said keep his commands because you love him (John 14:15), not so that he will love you. His love comes first, always, and obedience is how that love takes shape in a real life. Start with one command, obey it out of gratitude, and let the rest grow from there.
</WarningCard>

<PracticeCard label="Start This Week">
1. **Pick one command.** Choose one clear thing Jesus told his followers to do that you have been leaving undone, like forgiving someone or telling another person about him.
2. **Read the story behind it.** Find where Jesus said it in the Gospels, and read it in context so you know what obeying it looks like.
3. **Do it, and tell someone.** Obey that one thing this week, and tell another believer so they can encourage you and, better yet, do the same.
</PracticeCard>

## Frequently asked questions

### What did Jesus command his followers?

Many things, all summed up in love. When asked for the greatest command, Jesus gave two: love God with everything you are, and love your neighbor as yourself, saying all the Law hangs on these (Matthew 22:37-40). He also gave a new command to love one another as he loved us (John 13:34), and a final command to make disciples of all nations, teaching them to obey everything he commanded (Matthew 28:19-20).

### What is the most important command Jesus gave?

To love God with all your heart, soul, and mind. Jesus called this "the first and greatest commandment," and immediately paired it with a second like it, to love your neighbor as yourself, saying all the Law and the Prophets hang on these two (Matthew 22:37-40). Every other command he gave is one of these two lived out in a specific situation.

### How many commands did Jesus give?

There is no fixed number, because Scripture never counts them and much of Jesus' teaching can be grouped in more than one way. People who compile his commands arrive at different totals, often somewhere between about twenty and fifty. What matters more than the count is that he summed them up in love and expects them to be obeyed, not just known (Luke 6:46). Disciple-makers often gather about a dozen first commands as a place to start.

### What did Jesus command us to do?

To love God and love people, to follow him, and to make disciples who obey everything he taught. In practice, that begins with obeying his clearest commands one at a time: repent and believe, be baptized, love one another, pray, give, forgive, gather, and go tell others. See [what are the commands of Christ](/what-are-the-commands-of-christ/) for a simple starter set, each tied to a Bible story.
