<ArticleIntro>A three-minute testimony tells the same story a fifteen-second one does, in three parts: what your life was like before you followed Jesus, how you met Him, and what has changed. You just have more room to breathe. The work is filling three minutes with your actual story instead of padding, and stopping before it turns into a sermon.</ArticleIntro>

Usually someone asks you for this exact length. Your church wants a three-minute testimony before your baptism. A small group goes around the room. A mission team has everyone prepare one for the trip. Three minutes is long enough to really tell the story and short enough that nobody drifts.

Peter told the scattered church to keep this ready:

<ScriptureBlock reference="1 Peter 3:15">
But in your hearts revere Christ as Lord. Always be prepared to give an answer to everyone who asks you to give the reason for the hope that you have. But do this with gentleness and respect.
</ScriptureBlock>

That is what a three-minute testimony is for. Not a performance, an answer, ready before you need it.

## Three minutes, three parts

The shape does not change with the length. Before, how you met Jesus, after. What changes is how much room each part gets. Here is a rough way to spend the time.

<InsightCard label="A Rough Time Budget">
**Before (about 30 seconds).** Enough of your old life or heart to make the change believable. No more.

**How you met Jesus (about 90 seconds).** The heart of it. What you came to understand about Him, and what you did about it.

**After (about 45 seconds).** One or two things that are honestly different now, kept true to today.
</InsightCard>

Those numbers are a guide, not a rule. The point is where the weight lands. Most people get this backwards and spend two minutes on the wild before and fifteen seconds on Jesus. Give the change the most time, and give the turn the most detail.

This is not a modern invention. When Paul defended himself before King Agrippa, he gave what was essentially a three-minute testimony in exactly this order. Before, he violently opposed Jesus and threw Christians in prison. Then he met the risen Christ on the road to Damascus. After, he could not stop telling people to turn to God, "First to those in Damascus, then to those in Jerusalem and in all Judea, and then to the Gentiles" (Acts 26:20). Same three movements you are about to fill in.

## Where the extra time goes

If you already have a [fifteen-second version](/15-second-testimony-examples-ignite-your-faith/), you do not write a new testimony for three minutes. You expand the one you have. Take each of the three parts and add one true, concrete detail.

For the before, name the real thing instead of a category. Not "I was broken," but the specific fear or emptiness or habit you were living with. One honest detail does more than a paragraph of drama.

For the turn, this is where the extra minute belongs. Slow down. What did you actually come to see about Jesus? What did you do, whether that was believing, repenting, or starting to follow Him as King? If it happened over months rather than on one dramatic night, say that. Plenty of real turns are gradual.

For the after, resist the urge to promise a perfect life. Name what is genuinely different now, and be willing to say that some things are still hard. A testimony that oversells falls apart the first time your listener's life gets difficult.

If you have never written the story down at all, start with the composition basics in [how to write your testimony](/how-to-write-your-testimony/), then come back and stretch it to three minutes.

## An example, timed out

Here is an illustrative outline, not a real person's story, to show how the parts sit inside three minutes. Yours will sound like you.

> **Before (short).** For years I believed in God but ran my own life. From the outside things looked fine, but I was anxious most of the time and quietly tired of trying to be good enough for everyone, including God.
>
> **How I met Jesus (the bulk of it).** In my late twenties a friend kept inviting me to read the Gospels with him. I expected rules. What I found was Jesus actually forgiving people and calling them to follow Him, not clean themselves up first. Over a few months it stopped being an argument in my head. One night I finally admitted I had been running my own life badly, asked Him to forgive me, and decided to follow Him as King instead of just believing He existed.
>
> **After.** I still have hard days and I have not become an easy person to live with overnight. But I am not carrying the weight of proving myself anymore. When something falls apart now, I am not facing it alone, and that is the difference I did not have before.

Read that out loud and it lands right around the three-minute mark. Notice how little time the before takes, and how the turn gets room to actually explain what changed and why.

## Build yours this week

You can have this ready in one sitting. Give it twenty minutes and a timer.

<PracticeCard label="Build Yours This Week">
1. **Start from the short version.** If you have a fifteen-second testimony, put it in front of you. If you do not, write the three parts first.
2. **Add one true detail to each part.** The most to the turn, the least to the before.
3. **Say it out loud with a timer.** Not in your head. Out loud, timed. This is the step that shows you what is too long.
4. **Cut to fit.** Trim anything you stumble over, any word you would not use at a coffee shop, and anything that pulls attention off Jesus and onto you.
5. **Know where you will give it.** A baptism, a small group, a friend who asked. Prepare the room, not just the words.
</PracticeCard>

Step three is the one people skip, and it is the one that matters. A testimony you have only ever read silently runs long and tangles the first time you say it to a real person.

## What makes it go wrong

Once your three minutes exist, most of the work is cutting. These are the traps that turn a good testimony into an awkward one.

<WarningCard label="Watch for These">
**Going over time.** Three minutes asked for, six minutes delivered. If you were given a length, honor it. Time yourself and trim.

**Front-loading the drama.** Spending half your time on the rock-bottom story until Jesus is a footnote. Give enough of the before to make the change believable, then move on.

**Insider language.** Words like "convicted," "redeemed," or "backslidden" can make an outsider feel the story was not meant for them. Say the same thing in plain words.

**Turning it into an altar call.** Your testimony is your story, not a sermon. Tell what happened to you and let it open the door. Sharing the gospel itself comes next, and it is its own step.
</WarningCard>

That last one is worth holding onto. Your story and the gospel are not the same thing. Your before-and-after opens a conversation; the [good news about Jesus](/how-to-share-the-gospel/) is what actually saves, and it is what you walk someone toward once your story has their attention.

<PullQuote>Three minutes is long enough to tell the truth about your life and short enough to leave the other person room to ask about theirs.</PullQuote>

## Say it, don't file it

A three-minute testimony that lives only in a notes app has never helped anyone. The whole reason to prepare one is that you will be asked, and when you are, you want to answer well instead of rambling or freezing.

So finish it this week. Expand your short version into three honest parts, say it out loud with a timer until it fits, and line up the next place you will actually give it. When you are ready to say it in a real conversation rather than a prepared setting, [how to share your testimony](/how-to-share-your-testimony/) walks through delivering it naturally.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many words is a three-minute testimony?

Spoken at a normal pace, three minutes is roughly 400 to 450 words. Do not count words though. Say it out loud with a timer, because everyone speaks at a different speed and nerves usually make you talk faster. If it runs long, the "before" is almost always where to cut.

### What do I say in a testimony for baptism?

The same three parts: what your life was like before you followed Jesus, how you came to trust and follow Him, and what is different now. Keep the weight on Jesus and on the change, not on the details of your old life. A baptism testimony is a short, honest answer to why you are being baptized, not a full life story.

### What if I don't have a dramatic conversion story?

A quiet story still works, and it connects with more people than a dramatic one. "I always believed in God but was living for myself and felt empty, and then I actually started following Jesus as King, and that changed things" is a real before-and-after. You do not need a rock-bottom to have a genuine testimony.

### How do I keep it under three minutes?

Time yourself out loud and cut. The two most common reasons a testimony runs long are narrating the before in too much detail and adding a mini-sermon at the end. Trim both. If you can tell the [fifteen-second version](/15-second-testimony-examples-ignite-your-faith/) cleanly, the three-minute one is mostly a controlled expansion of it.
