Sharing the gospel is only half the moment. The other half is what you do with how the person responds. Most of us have a plan for saying it and no plan for the reply, so a real yes slips away or a maybe gets pushed into a corner. It helps to expect the responses in advance.

## Where the four responses come from

Paul preached the gospel to a crowd of thinkers in Athens, and in one short scene you can see the range of how people take it:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Acts 17:32-34">
When they heard about the resurrection of the dead, some of them sneered, but others said, "We want to hear you again on this subject." At that, Paul left the Council. Some of the people became followers of Paul and believed. Among them was Dionysius, a member of the Areopagus, also a woman named Damaris, and a number of others.
</ScriptureBlock>

Three responses in one afternoon: some said no, some said maybe, and some said yes. Add a fourth that you will often run into, the person who already follows Jesus, and you have a simple map for what to do next.

## Read the response like a traffic light

The easiest way to remember it is a traffic light, stacked top to bottom the way a real one is. Red means stop here for now, yellow means keep going carefully, and green means go. The fourth is the person already on the road.

<InsightCard label="The four responses and the next step for each">
🔴 **Red light (no):** not open right now. Next step: bless them and move on.

🟡 **Yellow light (maybe):** interested but not ready to decide. Next step: invite them into the [7 Stories of Hope](https://obey.tools/stories-of-hope.html) to explore Jesus for themselves.

🟢 **Green light (yes):** ready to follow Jesus. Next step: start discipleship right away with the [4-1-1 tool](https://obey.tools/4-questions.html) and the [commands of Christ](/what-are-the-commands-of-christ/).

**Already a believer:** a fellow worker in the harvest. Next step: help them grow and multiply, the same 4-1-1 and commands training.
</InsightCard>

The point is not to label people and lose interest. It is to give each person the next step that actually fits where they are.

## 🔴 Red light: they say no

Some people are not open, and that is where you bless them and move on. This is not writing anyone off. Paul did not argue the sneering crowd into the kingdom. He left, and some believed later anyway. Jesus told his followers to move on from a town that would not receive them, and to do it without bitterness. You keep the relationship warm where you can, you keep praying, and you spend your energy where God is giving openness. A red light today can turn green a year from now.

## 🟡 Yellow light: they say maybe

Plenty of people are genuinely interested but not ready to say yes. Do not push them toward a decision they are not ready to make, and do not drop them either. Give them a way to keep looking at Jesus for themselves. The [7 Stories of Hope](https://obey.tools/stories-of-hope.html) are built for exactly this: you read a short story from the Bible together, talk about it, and let them meet Jesus in the text over a few weeks. A yellow light is often a [person of peace](/finding-persons-of-peace-a-key-strategy-for-evangelism-and-disciple-making/) in the making, and a discovery process is how a maybe becomes a yes in their own time.

## 🟢 Green light: they say yes

When someone is ready to follow Jesus, the worst thing you can do is congratulate them and walk away. A decision is a starting line, not a finish line. Start discipleship right away, while the moment is warm. Get them into a simple, reproducible rhythm with the [4-1-1 tool](https://obey.tools/4-questions.html), and begin helping them obey [the commands of Christ](/what-are-the-commands-of-christ/) one at a time. This is where [making disciples](/how-to-make-disciples/) actually begins, and starting immediately sets the pattern for everything after.

## The fourth response: they already believe

Sometimes you share the gospel and find out the person is already a Christian. That is not a dead end. It is a coworker you did not know you had. The best next step is to help them grow, and especially to help them learn to make disciples themselves. That is the same path as a green light: the [4-1-1 tool](https://obey.tools/4-questions.html) and [commands of Christ](/what-are-the-commands-of-christ/) training, so that what you carry, they can carry to someone else.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the 4 responses to the gospel?

They are the ways people respond when you share the gospel, drawn from Paul's experience in Athens (Acts 17:32-34) plus one more you meet often. Green light is a yes, someone ready to follow Jesus. Yellow light is a maybe, someone interested but not ready. Red light is a no, someone not open right now. And the fourth is a person who already believes. Each one has a different next step.

### What do I do when someone says maybe?

Give them a way to keep exploring instead of pushing for a decision. A maybe is real interest, and the honest next step is a discovery process where they can look at Jesus for themselves, like reading the 7 Stories of Hope together. You are not stalling and you are not pressuring. You are walking with them while God does the convincing.

### How do I respond when someone says no?

Bless them and move on, without bitterness. A no is not a reason to argue harder or to treat the person coldly. You stay kind, keep the friendship where you can, keep praying for them, and invest your time where people are open. People change, and a no today is not final.

## Where to go next

Knowing the four responses only helps if you are actually sharing, so start there. Get the fundamentals down with [how to share the gospel](/how-to-share-the-gospel/). Then have the next step ready before you need it: the [7 Stories of Hope](https://obey.tools/stories-of-hope.html) for a maybe, and the [4-1-1 tool](https://obey.tools/4-questions.html) with the [commands of Christ](/what-are-the-commands-of-christ/) for a yes. Share, read the light, and take the next step.
