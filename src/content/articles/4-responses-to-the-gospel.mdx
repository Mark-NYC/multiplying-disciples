Not everyone you share the gospel with is going to believe. Jesus said so plainly, and he did not treat it as a failure. He told a short story about a farmer scattering seed and used it to explain why the same message lands so differently from one person to the next.

## Where the four responses come from

The four responses come straight from Jesus. In the parable of the sower, he described a farmer scattering seed on four kinds of ground:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Mark 4:3-8">
"Listen! A farmer went out to sow his seed. As he was scattering the seed, some fell along the path, and the birds came and ate it up. Some fell on rocky places, where it did not have much soil. It sprang up quickly, because the soil was shallow. But when the sun came up, the plants were scorched, and they withered because they had no root. Other seed fell among thorns, which grew up and choked the plants, so that they did not bear grain. Still other seed fell on good soil. It came up, grew and produced a crop, some multiplying thirty, some sixty, some a hundred times."
</ScriptureBlock>

Jesus explained it himself a few verses later: the seed is the word of God, and the four soils are four ways people receive it. The seed is the same every time. What differs is the ground it lands on.

## The four responses, one at a time

### 1. The hard path: it never gets in

The word is heard but bounces off. Before it can take root, it is gone. Jesus said the enemy comes and takes away what was sown. This is the person who is closed, distracted, or indifferent. You did nothing wrong. The ground was hard.

### 2. Rocky ground: a quick yes with no roots

This person receives the message with real enthusiasm, but there is no depth to hold it. When following Jesus gets costly, when trouble or embarrassment comes, they fall away as fast as they signed on. A quick yes is not the same as good soil.

### 3. Thorns: choked by the worries of life

Here the seed grows, but so do the weeds. The worries of life, the pull of money, and the desire for other things crowd it out until it bears no fruit. This person rarely says no. Life just slowly strangles what started.

### 4. Good soil: it takes hold and bears fruit

This person hears the word, holds on to it, and produces a crop, thirty, sixty, or a hundred times what was sown. Good soil is not marked by excitement. It is marked by fruit that lasts, and by a harvest that reproduces.

## The outcome was never yours to force

Notice what the farmer does not do. He does not stand over each seed and demand that it grow. He scatters, and the ground does what the ground does. Paul said the same thing about his own ministry:

<ScriptureBlock reference="1 Corinthians 3:6-7">
I planted the seed, Apollos watered it, but God has been making it grow. So neither the one who plants nor the one who waters is anything, but only God, who makes things grow.
</ScriptureBlock>

That is an enormous relief. Jesus himself got all four responses to his preaching, and he was the perfect messenger. If people rejected the word from him, someone rejecting it from you is not proof you did it wrong. You are responsible to sow clearly and love people well. The harvest belongs to God.

## What this changes about how you share

The four soils are not there so you can label people and move on. They change how you sow.

- **Sow generously, and do not pre-judge the soil.** You usually cannot tell good ground by looking. The person you assume is hardest can turn out to be the most ready, so share widely and leave the sorting to God.
- **Watch for the good soil.** As you sow, some people lean in. That receptivity is worth paying attention to. It is the same openness Jesus told his followers to look for in [a person of peace](/finding-persons-of-peace-a-key-strategy-for-evangelism-and-disciple-making/), and it is where your deeper time is best invested.
- **Do not mistake a quick yes for good soil.** Rocky ground looks wonderful for a week. Good soil is proven over time by fruit, which is why we do not just count decisions. We [make disciples](/how-to-make-disciples/) and watch for a harvest that lasts and reproduces.
- **Help people through the thorns.** Some who genuinely believe get slowly choked by worry, money, and busyness. Part of loving them is walking with them past the weeds, not just getting an initial yes.

<WarningCard>
None of this is permission to write anyone off. Soil can change. A hard path today can be broken up by loss or time, and the person who brushed you off this year may be the one asking questions next year. You read the ground so you know where to invest, not so you can give up on anyone.
</WarningCard>

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the 4 responses to the gospel?

They come from Jesus' parable of the sower in Mark 4, where the same seed lands on four kinds of soil. The hard path hears the word but never takes it in. The rocky ground receives it quickly but falls away when it gets costly. The thorny ground lets the worries and desires of life choke it out. The good soil takes it in, holds on, and bears lasting fruit. The seed is the gospel. The difference is how each person receives it.

### How do I know if someone is good soil?

By fruit over time, not by a first reaction. Good soil is not the person who gets excited fastest. It is the one whose life actually changes and keeps growing, who begins to obey Jesus and eventually helps someone else do the same. That is why we follow up, disciple people, and watch for a harvest that lasts rather than counting a single decision.

### Why don't more people respond to the gospel?

Because the response depends on the soil, not only the seed. Some hearts are hard, some are shallow, some are crowded with other things, and only some are ready to receive and bear fruit. Jesus faced the same range, so a limited response is not a sign the message failed or that you shared it wrong. Your part is to keep sowing clearly and to trust God with the harvest.

## Where to go next

The point of the four soils is not to predict people. It is to free you to sow.
