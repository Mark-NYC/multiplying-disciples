Most of us try to share our faith by pushing against closed doors. Jesus taught the opposite: go looking for the door that is already open, and go through it. That open door is often a person.

## What Is a Person of Peace?

A person of peace is someone who welcomes you and your message and tends to connect you to others. They are not necessarily a believer yet. What marks them is openness: they are glad to talk, curious about the things of God, and often eager to introduce you to the people in their life.

The idea comes straight from Jesus. When he sent his disciples ahead of him, he told them what to look for:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Luke 10:5-6">
When you enter a house, first say, "Peace to this house." If someone who promotes peace is there, your peace will rest on them; if not, it will return to you.
</ScriptureBlock>

The phrase behind "someone who promotes peace" is literally a "son of peace" (in Greek, a huios eirēnēs), which is where the term "person of peace" comes from. Jesus assumed his disciples would find such people, and that these people would carry the message further than the disciples could reach on their own.

## Where the Idea Comes From: Luke 10

Jesus sent out seventy-two followers two by two, ahead of him, into every town he planned to visit (Luke 10:1). Before he said a word about strategy, he told them to pray:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Luke 10:2">
He told them, "The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few. Ask the Lord of the harvest, therefore, to send out workers into his harvest field."
</ScriptureBlock>

Then he gave them a simple pattern. Enter a town, look for a household that welcomes you, and stay there. Eat what they serve, heal the sick, and tell them the kingdom of God has come near (Luke 10:5-9). If a place would not receive them, they were not to force it. They moved on and kept looking.

Notice what Jesus did not tell them to do. He did not tell them to convince everyone, argue down the skeptics, or stay until the whole town believed. He told them to find the open home and build from there. One welcoming household became the base for reaching a whole area.

## Why a Person of Peace Matters

A person of peace matters because they are rarely a single project. They are usually a doorway into a whole network of relationships.

The Bible is full of examples. The Samaritan woman at the well was not a disciple when she met Jesus, but she ran back to her town and brought it out to see him, and many believed because of her (John 4:28-30, 39). Cornelius, a Roman officer, gathered his whole household to hear Peter (Acts 10). Lydia, a businesswoman in Philippi, believed and was baptized along with her household, then opened her home as a base for the mission (Acts 16:14-15). Even the man Jesus freed from a legion of demons was sent home to tell his people, and he ended up proclaiming it across ten towns (Mark 5:19-20).

In each case, one open person opened a network. That network is what some people call an [oikos](/the-3-core-habits-of-a-disciple/), the household and web of relationships a person already has. The gospel has always traveled fastest along those existing lines of trust, which is why one person of peace can matter more than a hundred strangers.

This is not a reason to write anyone off. You care for everyone God puts in front of you. But you invest your steady, focused time where God is giving openness, because that is where the door is already open.

## How to Recognize a Person of Peace

A person of peace is not just someone who is friendly. Plenty of people are polite without being open to Jesus. Look for a deeper kind of welcome, which shows up in three ways.

<InsightCard label="Messenger, Message, Mission">
**Messenger.** They welcome *you*. They are glad to talk, they invite you in, and they keep the conversation going rather than shutting it down.

**Message.** They welcome the *gospel*. When the conversation turns to Jesus, they lean in instead of pulling back. They want to hear more, or they are ready to believe.

**Mission.** They open a *door to others*. A true person of peace connects you to their network. Like the woman at the well, they bring their people to come and see.

When you see all three, you have very likely found a person of peace.
</InsightCard>

<WarningCard label="What a Person of Peace Is Not">
**Not just a nice person.** Warmth is not the same as spiritual openness. Watch for real interest in Jesus, not only good manners.

**Not someone to use.** A person of peace is a person to love, not a target to work. If you only value them for their network, they will feel it.

**Not a reason to abandon others.** Focusing where God is working does not mean giving up on people who are slower to respond. It means going first through the open door.
</WarningCard>

## How to Find a Person of Peace

Finding a person of peace is less about a technique and more about praying, showing up, and paying attention. Here is where to start.

<PracticeCard label="Try This This Week">
1. **Pray for open people by name.** Ask the Lord of the harvest to lead you to the people he has already prepared (Luke 10:2). Write down a few names and pray for them this week.
2. **Go where people already are.** Spend time in your normal places, your neighborhood, your job, a local event, a place you serve. You are looking for people, not building a program.
3. **Start honest conversations.** Talk about real life, and be willing to mention your own faith. See who leans in when the conversation turns spiritual.
4. **Watch who welcomes you.** Notice the person who keeps inviting you back, wants to hear more about Jesus, and introduces you to others. That is the door opening.
</PracticeCard>

Prayer is where this begins, not a strategy. If you want a simple way to pray over the actual streets and people around you, [praying through your neighborhood on foot](/a-step-by-step-guide-to-prayer-walking-scriptures-and-changing-lives/) is a good place to start, and [praying specifically for the lost people you already know](/prayer-is-essential-12-key-prayer-points-for-disciple-making-movements/) trains your eyes to see who God is drawing.

## What to Do When You Find One

When someone welcomes you, the message, and opens their network, invest there. Keep showing up. Get to know their family and friends. And do not stay vague about Jesus.

At some point, tell them the good news clearly. Learn to [share the gospel](/how-to-share-the-gospel/) in an ordinary conversation, and keep a simple tool like the [3 Circles](/the-three-circles-gospel-presentation-step-by-step/) ready so you can explain it plainly. When they are ready, read the Bible together, and help them reach the very network they connected you to. A person of peace is often the first thread of a new group, which is how [making disciples](/how-to-make-disciples/) turns into something that keeps going.

<PullQuote>You are not trying to break down a closed door with enough persuasion. You are looking for the door God has already opened, and walking through it.</PullQuote>

Finding a person of peace is not a trick for closing a sale. It is a way of joining what God is already doing, one open person at a time.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a person of peace?

A person of peace is someone who is open to you and to the gospel, and who often connects you to a wider network of relationships. The term comes from Luke 10:6, where Jesus tells his disciples to look for a "son of peace" who welcomes them. A person of peace is not necessarily a believer yet, but they are receptive and willing to hear more about Jesus.

### How do I recognize a person of peace?

Look for three things: they welcome you (the messenger), they welcome the gospel (the message), and they open a door to others (the mission). A person of peace is not just polite. They show real interest when the conversation turns to Jesus, and they tend to bring their family or friends into it, the way the woman at the well brought her whole town (John 4:28-30).

### Does a person of peace have to be a Christian?

No. Many people of peace are not yet believers when you meet them. Cornelius, Lydia, and the Samaritan woman were all open and receptive before they came to full faith. What marks a person of peace is openness to you and to the message, not that they already believe.

### How do I find a person of peace?

Start by praying for open people by name (Luke 10:2), then spend time in the ordinary places where you already know people. Have honest conversations, mention your own faith, and watch who leans in and welcomes you back. You are looking for the person God has already made receptive, not trying to convince someone who keeps closing the door.

### What do I do after I find one?

Invest your time there. Build the relationship, share the gospel clearly, and read the Bible together when they are open to it. Because a person of peace usually opens a whole network, help them reach the family and friends they connected you to, so a group can begin rather than just one individual.
