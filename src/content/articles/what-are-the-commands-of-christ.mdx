That last line from Jesus is worth reading slowly, because it sets the whole target for following him:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Matthew 28:19-20">
Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you.
</ScriptureBlock>

Notice what the goal is. Not teaching people to know everything Jesus commanded. Teaching them to obey it. That distinction runs through everything he said. He never treated his words as ideas to admire. He expected them to be done. It is the whole reason [obedience-based discipleship](/obedience-based-discipleship/) exists: disciples are formed by doing what Jesus said, not by accumulating more to know.

<ScriptureBlock reference="Luke 6:46">
Why do you call me, "Lord, Lord," and do not do what I say?
</ScriptureBlock>

So if you have ever wondered what Jesus actually asks of you, you are asking the right question. Here is a plain answer, where to find the commands, and how to start obeying one this week.

## The Command Underneath the Others

Before you count individual commands, it helps to see the one command everything else grows out of. A religious expert once tried to trap Jesus by asking which commandment was the greatest. Jesus did not dodge:

<ScriptureBlock reference="Matthew 22:37-40">
Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind. This is the first and greatest commandment. And the second is like it: Love your neighbor as yourself. All the Law and the Prophets hang on these two commandments.
</ScriptureBlock>

Love God. Love your neighbor. Jesus said the entire Old Testament hangs on those two. Then, on the night before he died, he gave his followers one more, aimed at each other:

<ScriptureBlock reference="John 13:34">
A new command I give you: Love one another. As I have loved you, so you must love one another.
</ScriptureBlock>

Every other command Jesus gave is one of these three worked out in a specific situation. Forgiving someone is loving your neighbor. Being generous is loving your neighbor. Meeting to pray and eat together is loving one another. Keep those three in view and the rest stops feeling like a random rulebook. It is one life of love, spelled out in detail.

## Is There an Official List of the Commands of Christ?

Here people often expect a numbered list, the way there are ten in the Ten Commandments. There isn't one. Jesus gave commands all through the four Gospels, in sermons, in answers to questions, and in instructions to the people he sent out. The New Testament never stops to number them.

That means the people who have tried to count them come out with different totals, usually somewhere between about twenty and fifty, depending on how they group Jesus' teaching. So a "commands of Christ list" is always someone's arrangement, not a chapter you can turn to.

<WarningCard label="A list is a tool, not a new law">
A good list of Jesus' commands can help a new believer get started. It stops being helpful the moment it turns into a checklist you tick off to feel approved by God, or a fixed canon you argue over. Jesus already named the heart of it: love God, love people. Any list is only useful if it moves you to actually love and obey him, not if it becomes one more thing to know about.
</WarningCard>

## Knowing the Commands Is Not Obeying Them

Here is the trap the whole subject can fall into. You can read this article, look up every verse, and be able to recite the commands of Christ, and be no closer to obeying a single one. Jesus saw that coming. He ended the Sermon on the Mount with two builders, and the only difference between them was doing:

> Everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice is like a wise man who built his house on the rock. (Matthew 7:24)

His half-brother James said the same thing plainly: "Do not merely listen to the word, and so deceive yourselves. Do what it says" (James 1:22). For most of us the gap is not information. We already know we should forgive, give, pray, and tell a friend about Jesus. The gap is between knowing that and doing it, and that gap never closes by reading one more explanation. It closes by obeying one command, once, and then again.

<PullQuote>A command of Christ you have memorized but never obeyed has not shaped your life any more than a recipe you have read but never cooked.</PullQuote>

Obeying what Jesus said is exactly what marks someone out as [a disciple](/what-is-a-disciple/) rather than an admirer. He said it himself: "If you hold to my teaching, you are really my disciples" (John 8:31).

## Start With One This Week

You do not obey twelve commands at once. You obey one. Pick the next honest step and take it, then come back for the next.

<PracticeCard label="Your Next Step">
1. **Choose one command** from the twelve above that you know you have been leaving undone.
2. **Read its Bible story** and ask one question: what does obeying this look like for me, this week, with a real person?
3. **Do that one thing.** Small and real beats large and someday.
4. **Tell one other believer** what you are obeying and ask them to check in with you on it. Obedience was never meant to be private.
</PracticeCard>

There is one more command on the list that changes how you read all the others. Jesus told his followers to go and make disciples, "teaching them to obey everything I have commanded." That means obeying his commands is never only about you. As you learn to obey one, you are meant to help someone else obey it too. That is the whole move from following Jesus to [helping others follow him](/how-to-make-disciples/), and when a few people start obeying these commands together, they are already becoming [a simple church](/what-is-a-simple-church-meeting-christ-in-a-spiritual-family/). Walking a group through all twelve as a way of practicing into being a church is exactly what [commands of Christ training](/commands-of-christ-training/) is for.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the commands of Christ?

The commands of Christ are the instructions Jesus gave his followers in the Gospels, everything from "love your neighbor" to "go and make disciples." He summed them up in two: love God with everything you are, and love your neighbor as yourself (Matthew 22:37-40), plus a new command to love one another as he loved us (John 13:34). When Jesus sent his disciples out, he told them to teach new believers "to obey everything I have commanded you" (Matthew 28:20), so obeying his commands is at the center of following him. For the fuller picture of [what Jesus commanded his followers](/what-did-jesus-command/) across the Gospels, and how it all hangs on love, start there.

### Is there an official list of the commands of Christ?

No. The Bible never numbers Jesus' commands the way it numbers the Ten Commandments, and people who compile them arrive at different totals, often somewhere between about twenty and fifty. Any "commands of Christ list" is a helpful arrangement of his teaching, not a fixed chapter of Scripture. Disciple-makers often gather about a dozen first commands and practices a new believer can begin with, each tied to a Bible story.

### How many commands did Jesus give?

There is no single agreed number, because Scripture does not count them for us and much of Jesus' teaching can be grouped in more than one way. What matters more than the total is that he expects his commands to be obeyed, not just known (Luke 6:46), and that he tied all of them back to loving God and loving people (Matthew 22:37-40).

### How do I start obeying the commands of Christ?

Start with one. Pick a single command you know you have been leaving undone, read the Bible story behind it, and ask what obeying it looks like for you this week with a real person. Do that one thing, then tell another believer so they can check in with you. You grow into obedience the way you grow any skill, by practicing it, not by reading more about it (James 1:22).
